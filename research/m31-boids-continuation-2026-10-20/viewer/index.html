Can the flock keep moving?

We let local flocking rules steer the drones and feed each new airflow calculation back into their motion. This test asks whether that calculation remains usable for longer, retaining every prescribed path and any stopping condition.

Checking saved evidence…

Real computed motion; unverified physical model. These are saved states from coupled Boids calculations, not imposed position shifts. Passing a model check does not prove that real drones would fly this way. Each continuation starts from its exact saved state at original time 1 second, and is planned for four more seconds. Two original seeds and two step sizes are retained. The displayed clock starts at zero for this continuation; add 1 second for original time.

What this test can tell us

The paths contain local separation, alignment and cohesion, shared route guidance and a vehicle controller. This is a test of computational admission during coupled motion. It does not establish a new flocking method, real-world airflow accuracy, flight safety, speedup or large-swarm scalability. Comparing two step sizes is a sensitivity check, not proof of trajectory accuracy.

A refused RK trial stage is shown separately and is not appended to the accepted path. First refusal means first in evaluation order, not a precisely located physical event. Completed endpoints were not separately queried; the final endpoint may be unqueried. The initial positions, all trial states, controller commands and loads are preserved in the research record.

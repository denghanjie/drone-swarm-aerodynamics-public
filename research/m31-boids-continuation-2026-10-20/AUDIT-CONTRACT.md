# Saved-evidence continuation audit contract

20 October 2026. This is a new four-second continuation from the published one-second checkpoint; prior allocations remain closed. This document and `source/audit.py` are prospective source. They must be frozen, publicly byte-verified and included in the source manifest before the scientific producer runs. Writing or reviewing the source is not an allocation. The audit has not been executed merely because this source exists.

## Allocation and gates

There is one audit allocation: at most 1,200 seconds internally, a 1,220-second external process-group termination boundary, and at most 64 MiB of audit outputs. The external supervisor also requires its entire measured audit allocation, including process cleanup, to stay within 1,200 seconds. The supervisor launch lock and an exclusive new `audit/` directory prohibit retries. An error, timeout, incomplete input or output persistence error closes this allocation without permission to reclassify, tune or repeat it.

The audit uses Python's standard library only. It does not import or execute JavaScript, initialize drones, regenerate a Boids/controller decision, perform a native F query, solve a native root, make a network request, render the page or execute a scientific test. It reads the existing evidence and independently recomputes the arithmetic described below from saved binary64 words. It does not use the earlier exact-reference checker or its oracle modules.

Before examining trajectories it requires:

- A source manifest with unique safe repository-relative paths, byte lengths and SHA-256 identities. Symlinks and traversal paths are refused. Every pinned local file is checked.
- A source-publication receipt naming a full commit, the exact manifest hash, the exact verified file count, and both `remoteBytesEqualLocal: true` and `committedBytesEqualLocal: true`. These are the independently obtained public-verification receipt; the offline audit does not perform a second remote fetch.
- Manifest coverage of this auditor and contract, protocol, fixed configuration, runtime pin, producer, part-storage helper, storage qualification sources, motion wrapper, supervisor, display sources and inherited adapter/physics/simulation modules. Literal relative CommonJS dependencies are followed as text and must all be pinned. Pinned scientific modules are never imported. The earlier known adapter/native-load/physics/sim/grid/geometry SHA-256 identities must remain unchanged.
- The fixed N=64, two-seed, four-case configuration in the prescribed order; 800 or 1,600 steps at h=0.005 or 0.0025, controller cadence 0.01 seconds, four-second continuation horizon (relative 0–4; original 1–5), maximum 19,200 native calls and 1,600 controller updates, 3 GiB producer-output cap and 4 MiB raw record cap. The Node executable identity must be the prescribed pin in `runtime.json`; the frozen producer is responsible for checking the executable bytes before importing the scientific dependencies.
- A complete producer index with no failure, an internally closed zero-exit producer receipt, and an external native supervisor receipt with zero return code, no timeout, `allocationWallPassed: true` and at most 1,200 seconds. A scientific stop can belong to a complete producer allocation; resource, persistence, malformed output and unexpected exceptions cannot.
- A completed, passing `storage/qualification.json` with nonempty checks, plus `storage-supervisor.json` with zero return code, no timeout, `allocationWallPassed: true`, at most 20 seconds, `outputCapPassed: true`, and at most 8 MiB. This is the new package's one synthetic byte-storage qualification, not a scientific allocation. The audit reads its receipts and does not rerun it.
- The new native supervisor's `outputCapPassed: true` and exact `outputBytes` equal to the independently counted result-file bytes, at most 3 GiB. The later display gate must likewise require the audit supervisor's passing wall and 64 MiB output-cap flags.

The prior continuation checkpoint is fixed to public result commit `637c25294f162bea39970d12df0206f25dead889`. Its receipt at `research/publication-receipts/m31-boids-admission-result-2026-10-20.json` must identify the exact 20,563-byte, 100-pin prior result manifest with SHA-256 `a8fe6543894d1e5ddf3165d2b250304ef2d8c462f4e37f236b781312e02b974b`, with both byte-verification flags true. The current source manifest must pin that receipt and prior result manifest, plus the prior `results/index.json`, `audit/audit.json`, both internal exit receipts, native/audit supervisor receipts and `ALLOCATION-CLOSURE.json`.

Those selected prior files are hashed against the prior result manifest as well as the new source manifest. The prior audit must pass and report all four planned paths completed; its saved index/producer-exit/native-supervisor pins must match the selected files. Prior internal and external native/audit receipts must be successfully closed within their original 360/180-second limits. The prior allocation closure must show exactly one closed-passed attempt for native, audit, display and archive. Each prior case must have completed its corresponding 200/400 steps and original t=1 with no refusal. The new audit relies on this published prior audit for its already-closed corpus; it does not reread the roughly 500 MiB prior journals or require the entire old result manifest to become new source pins.

Each logical journal has no whole gzip file. Its index descriptor names the logical `.jsonl.gz`, aggregate compressed bytes/hash, record count, aggregate raw bytes/hash, and ordered `.part000`, `.part001`, … records with file/bytes/hash/offset. Each physical part is positive and at most 32 MiB, and offsets must be contiguous from zero. Only those parts and launch/index/exit files may be in `results/`; their total bytes must stay within 3 GiB.

The audit supplies an independent forward-only `io.RawIOBase` part reader to `io.BufferedReader` and `gzip.GzipFile`. It hashes every physical byte once in order while reading, checks each part at its EOF and the aggregate compressed stream at final EOF, and handles gzip members that span physical parts. Each decoded JSONL line updates a separate raw SHA-256 and byte count before parsing. The final raw identity and exact record count must match the index. No concatenated duplicate gzip or whole decompressed journal is materialized. It limits each raw line to 4 MiB, rejects duplicate keys and nonfinite JSON numeric literals, and checks contiguous sequence numbers. Sources, gate receipts, index and physical parts are hashed again before a passing report is produced.

The frozen writer groups complete raw lines into gzip members with at most 16 MiB uncompressed input before splitting compressed bytes into parts. The audit checks resulting byte identities and decoded lines; it does not infer member boundaries from record boundaries or separately measure each gzip member's uncompressed size. The prior synthetic storage qualification checks grouping and splitting behavior without scientific inputs. A buffered append is an accepted in-memory record until its block is flushed; complete case closure requires `finish()` to flush remaining blocks, sync/close each part and return the final identity. An incomplete producer allocation is rejected even if some buffered records were never persisted.

## Record order and outcome accounting

Each case has its own journal and therefore its own namespace for `coupled-stepN-kM` adapter identifiers. The auditor consumes the exact order: initial state; controller record whenever due; k1, k2, k3, k4; completed endpoint; endpoint safety record; then the next prescribed step or case-end. A saved structured native refusal terminates the current attempted step immediately and must be followed by case-end. There may be no remaining stages, endpoint, consumption or safety record for that incomplete step.

All state vectors must have the canonical IDs 0 through 63 and all 17 finite state words. Every continuation initial input must match all 17 words in the corresponding case's prior `finalInput` exactly, including signed zero. The two step sizes for each seed inherit their distinct published final states; they must not be reset to one common state. The initializer and first second are not rerun. The first controller update is due immediately at original t=1; subsequent updates use the fixed 0.01-second cadence. Original-time words are checked as `1 + relativeTime`. Restoring the initial record applies no extra quaternion normalization. Each case-end and its corresponding index entry must match the last completed endpoint, completed step count, attempted stage count, status, reason, refusal descriptor and nominal completed time. Global stage/native-call counts and compressed-byte accounting must agree with the journals.

Successful stages require one native call, completed adapter status, native-success status, saved admission true, saved descending graph eligibility, and saved residual diagnostics within their original thresholds. The audit checks these recorded diagnostics; it does not independently regenerate native admission geometry or residual-model evaluation.

A legitimate refused stage is a saved native `domain-refused`, `input-refused` or `numerical-failure`, coupled to adapter `failed` / `native-non-success`, zero consumed derivative rows, and its saved projected state. Its descriptor preserves status, reason, native phase, admission details, step/stage indices and relative time word. Such a refusal is a negative scientific outcome and may coexist with a passing evidence audit. Any other native status, thrown/malformed native evidence, failed endpoint projection or incomplete producer allocation is rejected.

An endpoint safety stop retains the newly completed endpoint, increments the completed step count and exports `safetyPassed: false`. The audit checks safety-record shape, IDs, finite recorded values and `pass`/issues consistency; it does not recompute obstacle or swept-contact geometry and grants no safety certificate. Native refusal retains the previous completed endpoint. Neither type of stop is silently converted to horizon completion.

## Binary64 consistency checks

Every scalar word is exactly 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits interpreted as big-endian IEEE binary64. Basic arithmetic is recomputed with Python binary64 values in the frozen JavaScript operation order and compared by words, retaining signed zero. This is a saved-arithmetic consistency check on this audit runtime. It is not an exact rational proof, arbitrary-precision reference, cross-runtime equivalence proof or aerodynamic/experimental validation.

Square-root results are treated separately. A recorded positive square root must be within one ULP of Python `math.sqrt` of the corresponding saved/recomputed operand. The recorded root is then used in downstream word comparisons. The report counts comparisons and one-ULP differences. The root-two constants are checked against the three corresponding permitted root-two candidates. This tolerance does not establish a correctly rounded exact-rational square root.

The unchanged adapter saves per-row norm-square/norm words on successful stages and completed endpoints, but not on a native-refused stage. For a refusal the auditor checks whether one of `math.sqrt(normSquare)` and its immediate lower/upper binary64 neighbors reproduces the complete saved projected quaternion. All non-quaternion words must remain exactly unchanged. The report explicitly counts this norm reconstruction; it does not pretend the omitted norm was directly recorded.

The checks cover:

- Controller state binding, cadence, step/time words, canonical neighbor-ID shape/order, finite desired velocity/target/quaternion/commands, saved target x/y binding and target-z arithmetic. Neighbor membership, steering, yaw `atan2`, actuator controller law and saturation decisions are not regenerated. Commands are then held and bound exactly through the prescribed integration stages.
- Every stage's raw trial state from the previous completed endpoint and preceding saved derivative: k1 base, k2 base + h/2 k1, k3 base + h/2 k2, k4 base + h k3. Stage, original-time and h words are checked. No extra endpoint query is inferred.
- Quaternion projection in all 64 rows, including norm-square operation order and exact non-quaternion preservation. Successful per-row raw/projected words must match the enclosing stage inputs.
- Both native input hashes against the exact compact insertion-order `{drones:[{id,state}]}` JSON serialization used by the adapter. Native raw inputs, canonical IDs and same-ID prepared centers/nominal forces must match the current projected state.
- Saved native constants; prepared body-axis rotations, rotor offsets, rotor points, nominal totals and square-root scales. Coefficient dimensions, row offsets, canonical nonself sources and successful positive ranges are checked. On successful stages, saved normalized coefficients are bound arithmetically to saved K/beta/nu. This does not regenerate the aerodynamic geometry coefficient K or validate its model.
- Successful native solution ranges; saved alpha sums from saved coefficients/y; rotor thrust product `nominal * x`; saved y from the delivered collective/nominal-total ratio under the square-root policy. The native cubic root algorithm and residual model are not rerun, and these checks do not validate native root accuracy.
- All four delivered rotor loads, collective left-to-right sum, world force, world moment and ID/status FNV32 consumption checksum. Each derivative row's consumed load must equal its same-ID native load word for word; no load from another stage, drone or endpoint may be substituted.
- Body-moment rotation, provisional nominal reaction yaw, total body moment, gyroscopic term and all 17 derivative words. Position rates, delivered acceleration/gravity, quaternion derivative, angular acceleration and motor lag use the frozen physics operation order and constants. The provisional yaw convention is checked as implemented, not endorsed as physical truth.
- The weighted RK endpoint `base + h * (((k1 + 2*k2) + 2*k3) + k4) / 6`, its subsequent quaternion projection and final input binding. A completed endpoint is a saved computed state; its native admission is only known if it is later queried as k1. The last endpoint may be unqueried.

The audit does not treat saved native admission counts, coefficient geometry, controller decisions or safety decisions as independently certified simply because their schemas and surrounding bindings passed.

## Export and descriptive observations

`audit/viewer-data.json` uses schema `m31-boids-continuation-view-v1`; the producer index uses `m31-boids-continuation-index-v1`. It pins the source commit/manifest and result index, carries the selected prior checkpoint provenance, sets `physicalValidity: false`, and contains all four cases in prescribed order. The export explicitly labels time as “seconds since continuation; starts prior t=1”. Every case exports its seed, dt, status/reason, completed relative and original horizons, completed/attempted counts, refusal descriptor and saved frames. `frame.time` remains relative 0–4; `frame.originalTime` is 1 plus that value. Each frame retains its journal sequence, step index, kind, positions in canonical ID order and safety flag. All 17 state words remain in the pinned logical gzip streams stored as ordered parts.

Frames contain only the initial state and completed projected endpoints, including a separately marked unsafe completed endpoint. A refused trial is exported only as `refusedFrame`, with its actual saved projected input, stage index, native status/reason and trial time. It is never appended to the completed trajectory. A first refusal is the first in discrete evaluation order, not a located continuous-time event.

Per-case `descriptiveMotion` reports the maximum center displacement and maximum absolute vertical displacement from the initial state to the last completed endpoint. It also reports mean center displacement and the maximum norm after subtracting that mean displacement from each drone's displacement. This is the COM-removed change of the equal-weight cloud. A case with no completed step has zero completed displacement; its refused trial is excluded. These descriptors do not establish flocking emergence, a causal explanation, physical validity or a continuous-time maximum.

Per-case `savedRegimeDescriptors` adds three prospective descriptors without new scientific queries:

- A controller neighbor relation is the directed ordered pair `(receiving drone ID, saved neighbor ID)` in that controller record. The first saved controller set establishes the baseline; each later controller record is compared with the immediately previous one in that same continuation case. Additions and removals are cardinalities of the two set differences, counted separately and summed over comparisons. The report includes comparison count, initial/final directed counts and the largest additions-plus-removals count at one update. It does not count the gap from a previous-stage controller record, infer unrecorded intermediate changes or regenerate neighbor membership.
- Saturation is the count of `saturated: true` vehicle command records divided, if desired by a reader, by the separately exported total vehicle command records. Each saved controller update contributes 64 records, even if its ensuing trial is refused. These are update/vehicle counts, not an integrated saturation duration or a re-evaluation of the actuator's saturation decision.
- `completedEndpointBodyAxisZMinimum` is the minimum z component of `rotate(saved quaternion, [0,0,1])` over all 64 drones and every saved completed endpoint, using the same ordinary binary64 rotation formula already checked by the audit. No further quaternion normalization, clipping or inverse trigonometric function is applied. The export identifies the first observed minimizer by ID, zero-based step index and relative time; ties retain the earlier observation. It separately reports `initialBodyAxisZMinimum`. With no completed endpoint, the completed minimum and location are null. A safety-failed completed endpoint participates; a refused RK trial does not. This is a numerical orientation descriptor, with no angle, physical threshold or safety conclusion.

Positive aerodynamic edge turnover is not computed; the export explicitly records this omission. None of these descriptors isolate a cause for earlier or continued motion, establish a new collective regime, or imply emergence.

`sameSeedComparisons` matches completed coarse step k to completed fine step 2k, from their distinct inherited continuation-start frames through the minimum available completed horizon. It separately reports the inherited initial maximum center difference and the largest per-drone Euclidean center difference including that initial discrepancy, the two saved time values at that difference, and the number of common endpoints. Integer step matching avoids false mismatch from binary64 time accumulation. No refused stage, extrapolated time or invented intermediate state participates. This descriptive time-step sensitivity is not a convergence-order or accuracy claim, even when both horizons complete.

`audit/audit.json` uses schema `m31-boids-continuation-audit-v1`. `passed` and `evidenceIntegrityPassed` refer only to this contract; `allPrescribedPathsCompleted` and `allAttemptedNativeStagesAdmitted` report separate outcomes. The report includes source/gate/index/logical-journal and physical-part pins, raw stream identities, selected prior checkpoint pins, counts, arithmetic scope/omissions, case outcomes, descriptive summaries and the exact viewer-export byte/hash pin. Logical journal filenames identify the concatenated streams; they do not imply a corresponding whole gzip file exists. A native-refused case is preserved, not hidden by a passing evidence audit.

`audit/launch.json` and `audit/exit.json` close the local allocation. Failure writes a false audit report (or a separate failure record after a late failure), exits nonzero and retains evidence. A viewer export from a failed or incomplete audit must not be used. Display requires the passing audit, matching export pins, internally closed zero-exit audit, and independently closed zero-exit external audit supervisor with `allocationWallPassed: true`. The external supervisor receipt is necessarily written after this script ends and must be enforced by the subsequent display gate.

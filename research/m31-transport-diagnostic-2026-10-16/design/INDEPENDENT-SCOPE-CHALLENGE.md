No invocation or resource allocation is authorized by this review.** It is justified only under the conditions below. Decline a proposal that merely renames, restarts, or supplies missing evidence to either closed body-load attempt.

## Why this can be a new question

The standing research mandate permits the coordinator to prioritize and adopt successive bounded questions with independent scrutiny. The campaign's `NEXT-DECISION.md:3` and `POSTRUN-RESTART.md:3` prohibit an automatic third runtime campaign, reopening closed budgets, and filling skipped stages. `NEXT-DECISION.md:14–20` separately proposes an instrumented transport-only diagnostic, explicitly without an existing allocation. These statements are consistent if a new experiment is independently justified and explicitly adopted before any exposure; remaining overnight time alone supplies no justification.

The unresolved instrumentation question is concrete: **for one fixed synthetic byte payload, under prospectively specified browser-to-sink transport conditions, which request, response, persistence, and acknowledgement events actually occur?** The old records contain a browser fetch error and an unstarted reservation but lack enough transport observations to identify the cause. Reliable evidence capture is a prerequisite for later numerical work. Measuring that boundary can therefore advance the main research program by characterizing an obstacle; it cannot satisfy the accuracy–computation or physical-validation objective itself.

The distinct estimand is observable transport behavior on synthetic bytes. The old estimand was complete field-to-body numerical output on scientific fixtures. Fresh names alone would not distinguish the experiments; exclusion of all scientific computation, old numerical buffers, and missing-output replacement is essential.

## Important limitation: synthetic memory is a different condition

The old browser source forms its body as a `Uint8Array` over a GPU-mapped range. A transport-only payload necessarily has different backing storage and omits the preceding GPU work. It may also omit timing and device-state interactions. Therefore even exact preservation of request semantics does **not** reproduce the complete old failure condition. A synthetic pass can characterize a working standalone route; it cannot eliminate GPU-backed body handling or earlier runtime state as competing hypotheses, and it cannot establish that the old upload would now work. This limitation should appear in the protocol before any result, not be added after a pass.

A single trial is adequate only for a narrow existence/event-boundary observation. It is insufficient for claims of reliable transport, intermittent-failure rates, performance, or a causal browser-version effect. No additional repetitions are implied by this limitation.

## Boundaries for the next design decision

- **One narrow mechanism.** Analyze only within-call reuse of successful exact beta/nu interval values first. Keep exact operands, interval algorithms, constants, operation order and result encoding unchanged. Define the cache lifetime as one immutable input method call. Use explicit source/receiver/rotor dependency identity; no cross-frame cache, approximate coefficient, previous-solution warm start or tolerance relaxation.
- **Preserve control flow.** Prefer lazy use at the original expression sites, so a zero/guard/refusal path does not eagerly evaluate a value it previously avoided. Show how exceptional/range behavior and branch priority remain unchanged; do not assume a certificate excuses altered premises. Prove cache values cannot be mutated by consumers. If this cannot be established from source, stop the optimization design before an empirical allocation.
- **Separate proof from cost.** The source design should identify the exact duplicated computation and its possible work-count reduction. Any later ablation must charge cache setup, lookup, memory, successful misses, teardown and all unchanged preparation/assembly/solve/certificate work. Counter reduction alone is insufficient. Source-level instrumentation or profiling needs its own declared boundary; no uncharged diagnostic pass is implicit here.
- **One fresh fixed corpus and two variants.** A modest future design could use at most two unexposed moving runs with a fixed full-state schedule, original attitudes/thrusts, one unchanged baseline and one reuse variant. Fix seed selection, frame count, support-density/nontriviality requirements, balanced timing order and finite repetition budget before exposure. Preserve every missing/refused/unresolved state in the denominator. Do not substitute a successful frame or tune geometry after observing failures. Include cache-specific bad-key/stale-state/equality controls in the newly frozen contract; this memo does not invent their tested outcomes.

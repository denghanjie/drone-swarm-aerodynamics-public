THE EXPERIMENT

Keep the selection. Change the omission.

C and Z make identical recorded selection decisions. C skips the selected contributions; Z evaluates them. Both pay for the bounds and selection. Z also pays a small, unquantified amount of extra reporting work, included in the comparison.

Z matched the independent full calculation within the frozen precision gates. C remained within the fixed load-error budgets. No flight, real-world physics, low-end hardware or browser speed was tested.

WHAT WE LEARNED

No useful incremental gain under this test.

The paired median C/Z cost was 100.382% for 1,000 drones and 99.345% for 5,000. Neither met the predefined 95% threshold with seven of eight C wins. Eight technical repetitions are not eight independent swarms.

The zero-screen package accounted for 90.27% and 92.42% of summed M-to-C savings. That package includes bounds and bookkeeping. This suggests testing cheaper exact-zero screening next; it does not prove approximation is always unhelpful.

## Timer interpretation and conservation

The reviewed original native wrapper and kernel timer placements support a nested partition, not addition of every recorded timer. The eight wrapper phases partition inner time. The four kernel phases are nested inside broad build/admission. Successful rows replace that broad wrapper bucket with kernelInput, kernelIndex, geometry, kernelAdmission and signed buildUnassigned, while retaining the other wrapper leaves. Signed outerBoundary and timerResidual complete an exact leaf sum equal to outer time.

The tolerance is exactly 0.000001 ms: absolute timerResidual may not exceed it, and buildUnassigned/outerBoundary may not fall below its negative. No clamp discards signed evidence. Other recorded phase intervals must be finite and nonnegative. Serialized decimal strings are handled exactly; this preserves logged rounded binary64 evidence and does not create additional measurement accuracy.

The labels and limitations now match the instrumented boundaries:

- `kernelIndex` means screening predicates and certificate checks, not spatial-index construction.
- `kernelAdmission` includes graph/reason checks, coefficient scaling and ordinaryLmax work.
- Geometry includes candidate `rowsFor` cache/list work, center tests, rotor interactions and row assembly/conversion. It cannot isolate cache cost or norm savings.
- BuildUnassigned includes wrapper preparation/evidence/validation and kernel work after t4, including return/assembly.
- OuterBoundary includes external owned copying, calls/returns and timing-object construction. It is not a pure copying estimate.
- Refused wrapper finalization includes the unclosed build interval and catch/output assembly, even if the kernel has returned. The renamed `unfinishedPhaseAndFinalization` preserves this meaning. Kernel timing is unavailable for this pattern, not zero.

Group reconciliation retains 216 method/group records, checks successful/refused counts and sums, and checks query totals plus the separately logged reset interval. F reset is a matched branch/timer interval, while C includes an actual reset. Reset is not assigned to success or refusal. `comparisonMs` is nested in the partial `encodePersistMs`; neither is added to the query partition or called complete process wall time. F strict-baseline comparison and C witness copying are asymmetric evidence work outside outer timing and may affect later cache/GC state. The protocol correctly limits interpretation accordingly.

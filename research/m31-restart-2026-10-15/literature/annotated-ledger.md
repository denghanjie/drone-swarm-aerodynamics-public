# Verified literature map for the M31 checkpoint

Audit date: 2026-10-15. Eight selected works; this is a bounded primary-source check, not an exhaustive novelty review. Bibliographic records are in [sources.bib](sources.bib); access and correction-search limits are in [source-access-log.md](source-access-log.md). “Full text” below means the listed relevant sections were actually read, not that every proof, figure, supplement or implementation was audited.

The checkpoint context is that M31 runs with airflow off and failed its numerical trajectory-agreement criterion. Literature cannot repair those results. No source below establishes this implementation's physical accuracy, WebGPU throughput, or operation of 5,000 coupled drones. The proposed follow-ups are [our experiments](next-experiments.md), not findings of these papers.

## Source findings and our use

**A1 — receiver measurements.** Fixed 4 V supply and hover PWM are explicitly distinguished from fixed rotor speed. Measurements separate thrust, pitch moment and PIV; offset and approaching/retreating cases differ. **Our inference:** prioritize a measurement-matched receiver response and offset cases, before many-drone validation. Access to the cited raw dataset was not obtained. Also, §III-A reports thrust *at* 65% nominal, whereas §IV describes a 65–70% reduction; this internal wording discrepancy is unresolved. Do not use the conclusion's percentage as calibration data. [A1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09463v1)

**A2 — inexpensive wake field.** The model concerns mean near-hover/far-field flow; its normalization uses motor distance, and the measured mass range starts at 230 g. Its controller example converts inflow into extra power through vehicle-specific throttle behavior. **Our inference:** use it as an explicit candidate field, with a separately identified receiver law. A 27 g vehicle, near field, overlapping wakes and unsteady turbulence require additional evidence. Preserve the exact length definition; do not interchange it with A1's center-to-motor arm length. [A2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13321v3)

**A3 — aggregation is an experimental question.** A powered receiver on a load stand measures interactions. Pairwise summation and nonlinear set aggregation are compared; the strongest nonlinear advantage is in vertical force, while several other axes are noisy or approximately linear. **Our inference:** pair validation cannot certify arbitrary wake superposition. Do not presume a nonlinear network wins every force or moment observable. [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03488v1)

**A4 — learned dynamics baseline.** Nominal rigid-body and actuator-delay dynamics are combined with permutation-invariant learned interactions. Although the general equations include torque, the reported implementation learns only vertical residual force (§VII-B). **Our inference:** this is a relevant force-model/planning/control baseline, not validation of our six-component aerodynamic response or local emergent flocking policy. Its bounded-error stability assumptions must not be treated as measured universal error bounds. [A4](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.05457v2)

**F1 — local 3D flocking definition.** The author's explanation specifies local separation, alignment and cohesion in a three-dimensional model, with distance/angle neighborhoods and optional obstacle/goal steering. **Our inference:** route guidance can coexist with local flocking, but a scripted formation alone is insufficient. These behaviors are established prior work; audit their implementation rather than claiming them as novelty. [F1](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/)

**F2 — constrained flocking baseline.** Acceleration-limited alignment and delays are explicit. Real experiments used 30 drones at the same altitude; the scaling paragraph presents simulation examples up to 1,000 without statistical detail. Walls/obstacles are virtual. **Our inference:** retain constraints and density-sensitive failure metrics, while avoiding equivalence claims with physical solid obstacles, fully volumetric flight or mutual aerodynamic validation. [F2](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/118802354/Optimized_flocking_of_autonomous_drones_in_confined_environments.pdf)

**C1 — direct GPU baseline.** Tiled all-pairs evaluation reuses data and avoids storing a complete pair matrix; arithmetic remains quadratic. **Our inference:** compare an optimized direct reference against approximations before attributing savings to physics simplification. CUDA gravitational-kernel timings do not predict WebGPU wake/control throughput, and gravity's pair symmetry must not be assumed for directed downwash. [C1](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-v-physics-simulation/chapter-31-fast-n-body-simulation-cuda)

**C2 — exact locality acceleration.** Uniform-grid binning with sorting or atomics supports neighbor enumeration. The fixed-capacity atomic example clamps counts; sorting avoids that fixed occupancy assumption. **Our inference:** preserve all neighbors required by the chosen finite-radius model and audit dense-cell overflow. Dropping nonzero distant wake contributions is a separate model approximation; merely indexing the same interactions does not change the physical model. [C2](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/assets/cuda/files/particles.pdf)

## Bibliographic corrections and remaining scope

Use the version read when citing A2; the arXiv record says 2024 while the [author laboratory](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/publications.html) lists RA-L 2025 and an older title variant. Use 2024 for A3's published chapter, with ISER 2023 explicit. Use 2022 for A4's journal volume, retaining online publication in 2021. These are date/version clarifications, not evidence of misconduct or a formal correction notice.

No novelty conclusion follows from these eight works. A broader search would still be needed for adaptive update policies, error-controlled wake truncation, GPU swarm simulators, rotor-level inflow response, wall/ground effects and recent learned surrogates. No independent physical dataset, source code reproduction or WebGPU benchmark was obtained in this literature task.

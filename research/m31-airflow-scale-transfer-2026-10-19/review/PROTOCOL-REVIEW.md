## Discriminating question and endpoint

One multiplier fitted on 8<=Z<12 must carry unchanged to 12<=Z<=30. The farther observations therefore test the transfer of that particular correction across the recorded spatial profile. A separate gain per height, lateral slice or evaluation band would remove that discrimination and is correctly excluded. No intercept or wake-coefficient search appears.

The corrected pooled-evaluation normalized RMS discrepancy is an appropriate primary endpoint for the stated scale question. Multiplying every measured u by the same positive constant multiplies the constrained fitted gain and corrected residuals by that constant, leaving their norm divided by the measured norm unchanged. This statement does not apply to raw-model errors or raw-to-corrected reductions with the saved predictions held fixed. The protocol correctly marks those as conditional secondary comparisons.

Calibration squared-error improvement is expected because gain 1 is feasible in the constrained fit; it is not evidence of transfer. Least squares does not guarantee MAE improvement. Adverse secondary changes must remain reported. A zero-clamped gain is a degenerate empirical correction, not a physically plausible positive reference-speed conversion. There is no calibrated accuracy tolerance, inferential threshold, uncertainty envelope or novelty claim. A remaining normalized residual exposes limitations of one constant correction on these recorded values, but does not uniquely identify wake shape as its cause.

The fixed bins have unequal cell counts and spatially correlated stitched observations. Pooled metrics therefore retain the selected equal-cell weighting, rather than giving equal weight to bands or treating cells as independent trials. Bin labels are selection intervals, not demonstrated coverage through their endpoints. Any later source-normalization investigation must distinguish the single-PIV field from other plotting workflows; a normalization discrepancy in another script does not establish an exact correction factor for this saved field.

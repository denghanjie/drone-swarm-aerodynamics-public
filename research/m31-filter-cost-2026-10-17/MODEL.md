# Unchanged model; scalar classification is the only method change

The raw-state adapter, exact pair law and coupled-load solver/certificate are byte-identical copies from the closed screening-cost package. The binary64 interval arithmetic and zero classifier are byte-identical copies from the closed filter-design package. Their local import paths resolve against this package's frozen constants and literals. The new method wrapper inserts zero classification before an otherwise unchanged scalar query; it makes no positive approximation and never changes the load model.

Prepared native binary64 values are treated as exact dyadic operands. The pair law retains finite support, guard/rejection priority and actual prepared axes/points. Both methods use the exact hierarchy and its global guard; a failed global proof falls back to scalar enumeration. The filtered method must still prove preceding guards and branch complements before returning a scalar zero.

For admitted ordered scenes the model is x_ir³ + x_ir Σ_j(a_irj y_j) = 1, with y_i = sqrt(Σ_r w_ir x_ir) and w_ir = f_ir / Σ_r f_ir. Predicted rotor thrust is f_ir x_ir. Collective thrust and world force/moment use the original prepared axes and offset cross-products. This is the declared hypothetical source-strength coupling, not a measured physical law.

The native 48-iteration/54-bisection proposal is untrusted until the unchanged fixed96 root-defect and complete load-box certificate, independent192 coefficient reference and independent128 root bisection checks qualify it. All four normalized load-error thresholds remain 1e-8. The independent oracle examines every logical pair, including pairs skipped by hierarchy or scalar filter. It is not enough for the two methods merely to agree.

All fresh scenes are upright lattices. No previous geometry-specific support bound is imported; generic wire caps are checked and resource failure closes the attempt. No current experiment covers general tilted/cyclic positive coupling, moving flight, physical measurements, or a complete 5,000-drone browser pipeline.

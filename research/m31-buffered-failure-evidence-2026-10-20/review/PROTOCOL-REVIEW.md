## Display assessment and resolved challenges

The viewer shows eight actual saved center arrays. It explicitly labels candidate success, candidate refusal and unevaluated status, starts on the refused state, and states that no large baseline comparison or speed result exists. It describes prescribed states rather than a flight simulation. The 186 figure is explicitly distinguished from drones and collisions; all centers are shown uniformly by whole-state status because exact offending pairs have not been identified.

The initial wireframe bounding box was removed because it could be mistaken for a model-domain boundary. The remaining box calculation is camera framing only. The protocol's geometry verification wording now refers to the Three.js Float32 position buffer: the QA reads that rendering buffer, compares all 24,000 coordinate components to `Math.fround` of saved values over eight states, and takes rendered screenshots. It is not a GPU-memory readback or a physical geometry audit.

UI checks cover page identity, limitation labels, all eight status/position buffers, orbit/reset, desktop/mobile overflow and screenshots, console/HTTP errors and refusal of corrupted view bytes. The owned browser process belongs to the UI supervisor's process group. Screenshots still require the parent's visual inspection; source review alone cannot establish successful rendering.

The export may write a passing audit body before a later deadline or finalization failure. Therefore finalizing display pins and launching UI must also require a successful export exit, elapsed export time at most 30 seconds, no export failure file, and the actual new-results payload within 10 MiB. A passing audit flag alone is insufficient. The reviewed finalizer/UI closure guards enforce this condition before display work. Display data and audit bytes are hashed after successful export, and corrupted data must be refused before a canvas is admitted.

## Claim and failure boundaries

Both failed native attempts must remain visible in the final history. The original stopped at a mistaken success expectation for an expected global-span refusal; the corrected attempt passed all small controls but stopped at a large candidate near-field refusal. The first failure does not disappear because its harness expectation was understood, and the second is not converted into a speed result by displaying its saved states.

The earlier physical-model limitations remain unresolved. F stays the baseline. Any future native experiment requires a separate prospective review; this stage authorizes none.

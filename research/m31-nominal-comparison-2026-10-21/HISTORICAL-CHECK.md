## Conditions for a meaningful comparison

1. Bind each new nominal path to the corresponding saved original t = 0 full 17-word state, not a reconstructed pose, a shared replacement state, or the coupled t = 1 endpoint. Verify exact initial identities. Each arm must update the unchanged controller from its own evolving states; copying coupled commands would define a different intervention.
2. Use the current adapter's existing `arm: 'off'` path. Source inspection confirms it constructs loads from the current nominal motor states and projected attitude and makes zero native calls. It retains nominal motor lag/reaction yaw and the common consumption/integration path. Zero native calls cannot establish aerodynamic admission along the off path, and nominal thrust is not a fixed-hover command.
3. Preserve the current projection arithmetic, controller cadence, geometry, inherited stopping rules and all prescribed cases. Do not substitute the older airflow-off generator or the older N16 correction adapter and label it unchanged. Runtime/source identities remain part of comparability; this historical review does not validate new producer code.
4. Treat the saved coupled baseline as t = 0–1 plus an exact-state t = 1 continuation, retaining its original-time labels and full-state boundary provenance. The continuation protocol explicitly disclaims bitwise identity to an uninterrupted five-second run. A new off path must document its clock/control scheduling and how common original times are matched; baseline reuse is not a new coupled rerun or independent replication.
5. Compare trajectories only on available common completed prefixes and report categorical terminal outcomes separately. Keep the accepted endpoint that triggered a safety stop distinct from earlier admitted native stages. Do not invent a five-second coupled endpoint for seed 2026102001 or infer an exact continuous-time contact time.
6. A paired difference estimates the total change produced by the implemented coupling intervention on these particular closed-loop paths, including subsequent changes to positions, neighbors and controller commands. It does not separately identify torque, collective force, individual steering terms, motor lag, or a controller cure. Both resolutions for a seed are correlated numerical paths, not independent statistical trials; small resolution differences are not physical error bounds.

## Recommended question and allowable claim

> For the two already exposed N64 initial configurations, does replacing the current coupled aerodynamic loads with nominal motor loads, while retaining the current projected dynamics and unchanged closed-loop controller, alter the recorded descent and terminal floor/vehicle-limit outcome over a prospectively fixed five-second horizon?

Describe a completed result as a **within-model nominal-versus-coupled sensitivity comparison on these exposed paths**, with the existing coupled outcomes reused. The historical record supports doing this bounded follow-up; it does not support saying that off/full effects, N64 nominal comparisons, or aerodynamic force/moment sensitivity have never been studied. No literature priority, physical validation, real-flight safety, general controller defect, optimal correction, large-swarm scalability or overall research completion follows from this comparison alone.

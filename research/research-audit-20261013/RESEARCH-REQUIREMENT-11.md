# Requirement 11: verified literature and tested applicability

Adopted 2026-10-13, after the numerical work in the exact-equation efficiency campaign. This is an additional research practice; it does not replace or renumber the original eight-step scientific roadmap.

## Required checkpoints

Consult relevant published research at campaign outset, before novelty claims, and whenever progress stalls, ideas run out, or experiments repeatedly fail. Before a paper, re-audit every citation and the closest prior work. Record the scope and date of each search: not finding an earlier method does not prove none exists.

When stalled:
1. Name the unresolved problem and the evidence showing it remains unresolved.
2. Search relevant methods, experimental results, failure analyses and adjacent fields.
3. Read and verify promising primary sources. Explain their relevance and limits.
4. Translate the relevant finding into a falsifiable hypothesis for this simulator.
5. Save a bounded experiment with controls, measurements, acceptance criteria and stopping conditions.
6. Test before claiming applicability. Preserve unsuccessful adaptations and disagreements.

Literature review does not replace experimentation. Published results support claims within their original conditions; a more detailed simulator is not automatically ground truth.

## Citation verification gate

Every citation must have verified title, authors, year, venue or preprint status, DOI or stable URL, and version consulted. Verify through a publisher, DOI record, official proceedings, institutional repository or recognized preprint repository. Read the source material supporting the attribution and identify its section, equation, figure, table or passage. Check corrections, retractions and newer versions where available; record the sources searched and the result, rather than declaring universally that none exist.

Search snippets and other models' statements are discovery aids only. If only the abstract is accessible, disclose that limitation and make no claims about inaccessible details. Record blocked access and seek legitimate alternatives. Exclude references or attributed findings that cannot be verified. Never invent bibliographic details, quotations or findings.

## Repository records

Maintain a machine-readable bibliography and a literature–experiment ledger. Put citations beside the claims they support, not solely in a bibliography. Cite every external work materially informing algorithms, physical assumptions, parameter choices, datasets, experimental methods or interpretation.

Each ledger entry records:
- Verified bibliographic details, publication status, version and stable link.
- The specific idea used and its exact source location.
- Source assumptions, limitations and accessible-material limits.
- Relationship: reproduced, adapted, inspiration only, related comparator, or unverified provenance.
- Implementation commit and exact source digest; experiment IDs and record links.
- Our results, failures, disagreements, and unresolved applicability.
- Verification date and correction/new-version checks.

Push reviewed ledger and bibliography updates at important checkpoints when repository publication is authorized and available. Keep publication failures explicit. Do not erase frozen experimental plans to make a retrospective review appear prospective.

## Current audit boundary

The exact-equation campaign was completed before this requirement was received. Its first literature ledger is retrospective. Existing experiment records remain unchanged. Any newly proposed literature-inspired method remains untested until a separate bounded experiment is run. Historical citations outside the audited subset remain unaudited and cannot support a paper's completeness or novelty claim.

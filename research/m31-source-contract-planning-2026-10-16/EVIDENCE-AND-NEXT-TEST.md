# Motor-responsive source contract: evidence and one next test

2026-10-16. Literature/source inspection only. No campaign, fitting, scoring, digitization, numerical-model execution, author contact or environment change. The runtime replication is separate. Two new literature leads were pursued; only Wolf et al. was actually verified and added to the bibliography. Existing Brown and excluded Neural-Swarm downloads were not revisited.

**Decision:** published evidence supports testing a steady thrust-scaled wake as a restricted hypothesis. It does not establish a calibrated command-to-wake-and-receiver contract for the 27 g vehicle. The next physically defensible checkpoint is a single-source scaling falsification with a named actuator condition and independent load/flow evidence, not another calibration of the existing hover-source implementation.

The quantities must remain distinct:

| Quantity | Contract needed |
|---|---|
| Command/PWM and nominal motor state `f0` | `f0` is the simulator's nominal thrust state. A command or first-order lag is not a measured RPM, shaft power or effective thrust. A physical mapping needs identified hardware, supply and actuator/control behavior. |
| RPM `n` | Measured or regulated rotational frequency. Under external inflow, fixed RPM need not preserve thrust or power. |
| Shaft power `Pshaft = 2πnQ` | Requires shaft torque `Q`; electrical `VI` also includes drive losses. Neither is automatically ideal disk power. |
| Effective thrust `T` | Actual aerodynamic output under the current inflow and actuator state. It is the candidate momentum source, not automatically the still-air nominal thrust. |

Inherited evidence was consulted first. In [Bauersfeld et al., arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13321v3), freshly reread §§III-B/D and VII-A, Eq. (1) gives the hover scale `UH = sqrt(TH/(2ρAprop))`, with equal-rotor `TH = mg/4`; Eq. (7) normalizes velocity by that scale. Eqs. (10)–(11) calculate extra ideal aerodynamic power needed to preserve hover thrust in downwash, followed by a vehicle-specific throttle adjustment. They do not establish constant ideal power at fixed command. The field is a mean near-hover/far-field model, with measured vehicles starting at 230 g. Substituting arbitrary motor-dependent thrust into its normalization is our proposed extension, not its demonstrated general result.

Neither supplies the intended nanoquad's simultaneous source flow, effective thrust and fixed-command receiver law. **One falsifiable next experiment, proposed only:** test whether one fixed mean-wake shape admits thrust-only amplitude rescaling on the identified intended source hardware, in steady still air outside ground effect. Use an independently measured symmetric baseline `(T0, ρ0, ū0(x))` and predict held-out conditions by

`ūpred(x) = ū0(x) sqrt[(T/ρ)/(T0/ρ0)]`.

This is our explicit hypothesis, not a published universal law. Use one lower symmetric command and one unequal command vector selected by independent thrust-only calibration to match the baseline total thrust; lock those commands before inspecting flow. Include an all-off, fully settled background measurement. Measure total aerodynamic thrust independently of the flow reconstruction, motor commands, RPM, supply and a preselected downstream/radial flow profile; record torque if making a shaft-power claim. Separate repeatability, sensor calibration, support effects and registration uncertainty. A resolved change of normalized profile or spatial asymmetry on either held-out condition rejects scalar rescaling over that domain. Predeclare profile contrasts, simultaneous uncertainty coverage and the application adequacy tolerance; do not borrow f32 gates as measurement tolerances. Do not refit the shape on the same test cases. Passing would support this finite steady source contract only.

The precise data gap is an identified propeller/drive's independently measured thrust and spatial flow across those locked actuator conditions, with usable repeats, uncertainty and reuse terms. Current retrieved material does not fill it. Near-zero transient wake decay, arbitrary unequal-rotor fields, receiver losses and swarm dynamics remain separate. Fully settled zero powered-source contribution is a constraint; instantaneous disappearance after shutdown is not assumed.

For subsequent receiver coupling, write an explicit `Tj = F(actuator_j, Uj)` under the verified control condition. The current constant ideal-disk-power closure `P* = T[U/2 + sqrt(U²/4 + T/(2ρA))]` is not identified with shaft power or fixed PWM by these sources. If a source uses effective `Tj` and `Uj` depends on other sources' effective thrusts, cycles in the influence graph can create an implicit coupled system. Existence, uniqueness, solver error and cost then require new analysis. Using prior-step thrust instead is an explicit temporal approximation, not a free solution of the same contract.

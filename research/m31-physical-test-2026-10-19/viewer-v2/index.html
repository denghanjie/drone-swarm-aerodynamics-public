What this can tell us

The model sends the aerodynamic influence downwards. It therefore predicts no change in the upper drone’s lift when the lower one moves farther away. The experiment lets us challenge that assumption directly.

The lower-drone calculation also assumes that keeping the same motor command can be represented by our simplified force rule. Any discrepancy challenges that whole approximation; it does not identify one formula as the sole cause.

This is a limited physical comparison, not proof of safe swarm flight or a new discovery. The authors already reported upper-drone effects. Our contribution here is checking our own model against their accessible measurements.

Methods, uncertainty and all saved cases

11 stacked placements, 25–110 cm. The 13, 17 and 21 cm points are outside the model’s near-field coverage under the figure-supported motor geometry. No force values were used to choose these cases. Fixed voltage and motor command are not fixed power.

Calibration, mounting uncertainty and differential drift are not fully quantified in this release. Published SD values are retained in the data, but are not treated as uncertainty of the mean. The sensitivity grid varies input assumptions; its range is not a statistical interval.

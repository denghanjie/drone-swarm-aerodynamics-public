## Lifecycle blockers found and resolved before sealing

The initial wrapper (`ce2b913850f85cb7a0fe0f2c7c3c51c9dc27a1add191bc0289cf86428acbbc4f`) enforced its deadline only while the Node child ran. Synchronous preflight, post-child hashing and final receipt writing could escape that enforcement, while a pre-write elapsed sample could be mistaken for completed supervisor closure. This was a blocking static finding and was communicated before any reviewer execution.

The accepted wrapper starts its monotonic accounting before remaining imports and arms soft/hard SIGALRM handling before output reservation and scientific preflight. The soft threshold is 118 seconds; the internal hard threshold is 120 seconds. The checker also has a cooperative 110-second limit. The permanent lock prevents another launch. The fixed Node child stays in the wrapper's coordinator-owned group and spawns no descendants. The wrapper kills/waits its exact child when needed and records whether it was reaped. Finalization remains under the internal alarm. Its terminal budget always has `integrityVerified=false`; a candidate requires a completed successful checker, no errors/stops, reaped child, the timing sample below 118 seconds and sufficient output reserve. A partial receipt, failed exit or candidate flag alone cannot establish completion.

A second boundary defect was found in an intermediate completion-sealer revision: its internal two-second timer started after interpreter startup/imports, yet it asserted that wrapper wall plus two seconds bounded the complete lifecycle. That claimed upper bound was removed. The accepted helper explicitly describes its timer scope, keeps `integrityVerified=false`, and records `candidateIntegrityVerified` together with `externalCompletionObservationRequired=true`.

The accepted implementation memo requires one external monotonic chain covering the wrapper and completion subprocesses, including interpreter startup, imports, final writes and closure observation. The coordinator must create each command's session/group, apply a timeout within the remaining 120-second chain budget, retain time for bounded cleanup, kill and wait the owned group on timeout, and treat any stop or unconfirmed closure as failure. The Node child is not detached, so stopping the wrapper's owned group also contains that child. On success the coordinator must observe both exits 0, absence of both owned groups, and actual total chain time below 120 seconds. These external observations are part of the acceptance predicate, not facts either subprocess can certify about its own future exit.

The completion mode is byte/lifecycle administration only. It does not call the numerical checker again. It requires caller-observed wrapper exit 0 and wall below 118 seconds; validates the prior five-file inventory and totals; binds the review freeze to the wrapper-recorded freeze and science-manifest identities; and checks the worker's source identity and all required candidate/failure flags. Missing, malformed or contradictory evidence cannot qualify. Its exclusive `completion.json` self-accounts its serialized bytes, stays within the reserved 4,096 bytes, and supplies the six-file total while preserving the separate 16,384-byte observer-provenance reserve under the 32 MiB cap. The wrapper budget remains the pre-completion five-file account.

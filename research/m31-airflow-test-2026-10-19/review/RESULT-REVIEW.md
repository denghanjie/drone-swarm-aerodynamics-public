## What follows, and what does not

The comparison isolates an airflow-component discrepancy without invoking a receiver cubic or thrust-response model. Under the project's adopted downward-axial interpretation and the assumed shared Ui=UH convention, the current kernel overshoots the released mean axial field on this fixed sample. Adjusting the receiver law alone would not remove that already observed airflow comparison error.

Its cause is not uniquely identified. Ui and UH use compatible induced-speed formulas, but this allocation did not establish the same calibrated thrust/operating point. A's amplitude discrepancy remains conditional on that scale mapping. The original Bauersfeld probe measured speed magnitude, whereas u_piv is a signed mean axial component. The measured rotor-row plane and the model's axisymmetric assumption are also distinct. The result cannot by itself separate coefficient/width errors, normalization errors, observable differences, and departures from that spatial assumption.

B is an in-sample published reference, not an independent challenger. Its smaller error is useful diagnostic context, but its nonzero MAE and negative bias prevent calling this an exact reconstruction agreement. A fitted analytic curve is not expected to reproduce every measured cell exactly, so these residuals alone do not prove a wrong implementation, a wrong paper, or a defective dataset. The independently checked calculation agrees with the declared formula; correspondence between that formula, the released aggregate field, and the publication's fitting procedure remains a separate source-reconstruction question. The known 2026 README/release versus the original 2025 figure-generation bytes is one unresolved provenance distinction, not an established explanation of the residual.

No experimental uncertainty envelope or independent calibration was supplied. The arithmetic tolerance 2e-12 Ui is not a physical accuracy allowance. No significance level, pass/fail physical tolerance, generalization performance, flight safety, or full three-dimensional validity follows from these summaries.

## Design reasoning

The local coordinate argument is correct: one horizontal component has a nontrivial radial/azimuthal ambiguity; two colocated horizontal components in a known registered frame invert a determinant-one rotation at R>0. Measuring the third Cartesian component enables the axial comparison. This does not require assumed symmetry or zero swirl and does not reconstruct an absent component. The right-handed frame and downward axial sign are explicit.

The coarse analytic bound places the proposed dimensionless rings inside xi<2 under the inherited width definition. It is a support-design argument, not a new wake evaluation or evidence that an unidentified real airframe is compatible. The fixed heights, rings and angles are transparent engineering choices; the 48 sites are neither a power calculation nor independent repeats.

The earlier challenge is materially addressed. Independent pose/axis registration and uncertainty are required; fitting a center to improve agreement is excluded. Sequential sections require stationarity/drift evidence and a declared acquisition order. The sin(8phi) counterexample correctly demonstrates finite-angle ambiguity. Sampled agreement cannot prove global axisymmetry; mean-zero tangential velocity cannot rule out instantaneous motion. Three heights alone do not establish resolved derivatives or a complete continuity test.

## Measurement and inference gates

The design correctly separates calibrated observation checks from a physically scaled prediction comparison. Stereo three-component plane measurement is distinguished from ordinary two-component planar measurement and simultaneous volumetric observation. Literature establishes methods and uncertainty considerations, with access limits retained; it does not certify apparatus availability, optical access, precision or feasibility.

The data contract requires directly observed signed Cartesian components, effective support, calibration/processing lineage, registration and shared error relationships. Repeated frames or spatial nodes cannot be counted as independent runs. Shared calibration uncertainty does not disappear through repetition. The complete primary claim requires all 48 sites to pass prospective quality rules; missing data cannot be filled from the model, discarded by residual or turned into an all-site pass.

The interval logic is appropriate conditional on a justified uncertainty procedure: containment within an independently fixed tolerance supports agreement at that precision; separation on one side supports material mismatch; mixed acceptable/unacceptable coverage is inconclusive. Containing zero alone is not acceptance. Multiplicity, correlated uncertainty, systematic error, margins and replication remain prospective requirements. Statements about swirl or sampled symmetry must be read as uncertainty- and tolerance-resolved findings, not conclusions from any nonzero point estimate.

Physical model amplitude remains a separate gate. The inherited empirical gain is not an SI conversion; no target-fitted gain or geometry adjustment is authorized. Calibration failure is not model failure, and a calibrated discrepancy would not identify a unique physical cause.

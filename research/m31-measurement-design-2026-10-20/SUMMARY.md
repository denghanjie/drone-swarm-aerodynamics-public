# We now know what a useful test must measure

The design separates three questions:

1. **Can we trust the ruler?** Calibrate velocity and positions against an independent reference, not against our model.
2. **Which way is the air moving?** Measure both sideways directions and downward motion at the same location. One sideways reading can mix outward flow with swirl.
3. **Is the rest of the wake similar?** Repeat those measurements around the wake and at several heights. A few locations cannot prove that the whole wake is symmetrical.

The candidate layout has 48 planned locations: two rings at each of three heights, with eight positions around each ring. These are places to measure, not 48 independent experiments. The 3D page illustrates that arrangement; its arrows show directions only, not simulated air speeds.

**This is a test design, not a new experiment or breakthrough.** We still lack a confirmed compatible acquisition, instrument uncertainty and independently justified physical scaling for the model. Those gaps prevent a defensible physical test today. The plan states what outcomes would support agreement, show mismatch or remain inconclusive, but does not invent thresholds or repetition counts.

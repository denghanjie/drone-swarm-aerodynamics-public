# Browser-screen question: alignment with the research goal

**Assessment:** the proposed browser confirmation is a useful, bounded implementation and process-robustness question. It advances part of original roadmap Step 6. A successful result would support a faster implementation of the specified hypothetical interaction model on the named browser and tested states. It would not complete the 5,000-drone coupled browser objective or establish the physical accuracy axis, a new algorithm, or publication readiness.

This review used only the 12 content files listed below: the new prospective scope plus 11 existing source notes, summaries, and ledgers. No raw target arrays, scientific code imports, experiments, numerical recomputation, external source search/retrieval, new agents, or repository edits were used. Numbers reported here are copied from existing prose. The only output is this planning review. No source-access or execution allocation is created or reopened.

## What the proposed question contributes

The original roadmap asks which interaction approximations preserve specified physical predictions while allowing locally controlled 3D flocks to run within measured WebGPU browser budgets toward 5,000 drones [D2]. Its core contribution is a measured accuracy–computation comparison. The factory is the workload; successful passage, a replay, or a component benchmark is not the complete research result [D2–D3].

The completed screen is evidence for a finite cost improvement without changing checked model outputs: the two N5000 cases passed, the N1000 case lost, one primary timing pair lost, and variability remained substantial [D11]. These are important reasons to test the unchanged intervention in actual browser JavaScript and fresh processes. The proposed three sequential browser launches address process dependence that the earlier single Node process could not examine [D1, D11].

The proposed new seeds and five-second inputs improve prospective confirmation, but they remain short, same-family, airflow-off states. They do not sample an independently justified physical operating envelope, test mature flock navigation, or exercise loads through feedback motion. Fresh processes replicate runtime conditions; they do not turn three input states into a population of independent swarm trials. If the selected browser shares V8 with Node, that also limits how much engine diversity has been tested. Any claim should name the actual browser, version, hardware, wrapper, and admitted domain.

| Decision | What browser confirmation can establish | What remains outside it |
|---|---|---|
| Retain F for this browser workload | Finite L/F output agreement, control behavior, and a complete-query timing benefit under a frozen browser schedule | General floating-point equivalence, every state, other devices/engines, or an asymptotic improvement |
| Characterize process dependence | Per-process timing distributions, adverse pairs, launch failures, and whether the same rule passes consistently | A population confidence claim from pooled correlated repeats |
| Prepare browser integration | Whether unchanged numerical source plus a transparent wrapper operates under the declared interface; explicit adaptation and artifact costs | Controller/integration/render/transfer throughput or real-time coupled flight |
| Choose subsequent optimization work | A measured baseline for current-query cost, including preflight and fallback costs within the query contract | A GPU speedup, a valid stale-state refresh policy, or a physically justified approximation |
| Assess the publication contribution | A reproducible engineering result and bounded counterexamples | Physical validation, matched superiority to external methods, or novelty by itself |

The prospective scope correctly excludes generation, transport/serialization, and rendering from matched query timing while requiring them to be accounted separately [D1]. Its final protocol should preserve that distinction and state whether browser-wrapper allocation/copying belongs inside the query. Otherwise a changed measurement boundary could obscure the meaning of a timing gain. A query-only pass should not be relabeled a live simulation result.

## The physical-validity gap is larger than runtime confirmation

The source-law provenance is conditional, not an experimentally identified actuator-to-load chain [D4, D9]:

1. The inherited Bauersfeld mean-wake form uses a hover scale based on thrust and disk area. The verified reading covers near-hover/far-field mean flow and measured vehicles starting at 230 g. Applying it to the 27 g vehicle, substituting arbitrary motor-dependent effective thrust, and retaining a fixed shape are extensions or assumptions of this project [D4]. Correctly implementing that form cannot verify those extensions.
2. Nominal simulator thrust state, PWM/command, RPM, shaft power, ideal disk power, and effective aerodynamic thrust are distinct quantities. The current constant ideal-power receiver closure is not established as a fixed-PWM or measured shaft-power law. Wolf's free-hover results involve flight control and varying rotor speeds; they do not identify the intended nanoquad's fixed-command response [D4].
3. The later coupled model uses assumed source-total strength and receiver relationships. Its numerical solver agreement and eligible ordering in four historical states establish conditional computational facts, not the physical law or general acyclic ordering [D9]. Cyclic effective-thrust coupling, altered attitudes, and control-dependent source behavior remain separate issues.
4. Aggregation, receiver force/moment response, and coupled dynamics need their own compatible evidence. Single-source agreement does not establish arbitrary overlapping-wake superposition, and local numerical load agreement does not establish trajectory accuracy under feedback [D2–D4, D9, D12].

The physical-source admission is **closed and unresolved**. The latest fixed-PWM report view consumed exactly 3 of 3 permitted connector operations. It did not expose readable marker/curve identities and calibration sufficient for extraction, and no thrust triplet or numerical comparison was admitted [D5]. Matching PWM alone does not establish a common specimen/run/static baseline, installed-load/tare treatment, actual pressures, repetitions, or complete uncertainty. The retained 16-inch-to-46-mm hardware transfer and fixed-command-versus-fixed-power gaps remain. This is an access/admission result, not proof that compatible data do not exist. Browser success does not reopen it.

The source-profile reanalysis also cannot supply the missing validation: it found close displayed centerline medians but a radial-profile discrepancy in reconstructed plotted curves, without full measurement uncertainty or a justified adequacy threshold [D6]. It supports the warning that centerline agreement alone is insufficient; it does not physically accept or reject hover normalization for the swarm task.

The highest-value physical question remains identification and falsification of the intended source/actuator contract with independent thrust and spatial-flow observations, followed by receiver and multi-wake tests. The already recorded source-scaling proposal specifies held-out actuator conditions, independent thrust, spatial contrasts, and uncertainty [D4]. It remains a proposal, with no new acquisition granted here.

## Existing browser findings do not close the coupled pipeline

The earlier WebGPU receiver component returned all 24,032 valid rotor forces within a chosen 1e-7 N engineering allocation; the largest reported error was 8.564453909953862e-9 N. The older 1e-12 N criterion failed for 24,022 inputs. Those are compatible findings at different declared precision requirements, not evidence that GPU and binary64 implementations are interchangeable. The tested inputs were openly reused; the allocation was not derived from experimentally established flight tolerances. This component supplied no speed comparison or physical validation [D7, D10].

The subsequent wake slice compiled and produced 80,000 valid, class-matched term records, but one term-error gate failed. Its reduction was consequently skipped. Thus there is no completed wake-accumulation result from that experiment, and API success cannot be substituted for numerical passage [D8].

The proposed browser JavaScript screen is a distinct CPU-side query question. It does not repair or retest that GPU wake failure, and it does not combine separately passing pieces into an end-to-end success. The closed component findings should remain visible in the research narrative.

## Meaningful accuracy–computation and novelty requirements

There are two different accuracy axes to retain. **Numerical fidelity to the specified model** can be measured now with exact L/F comparisons, independent references, declared tolerances, and complete cost accounting. **Physical predictive accuracy** needs independent compatible observations, uncertainty, held-out conditions, and application-specific observables. A faster evaluation with the same outputs moves the cost coordinate of a fixed model; it does not establish a new physical accuracy–cost frontier [D2–D4, D11]. A strictly computational paper would need to state that narrower target and establish its contribution against appropriate computational baselines rather than borrow the unverified physical claim.

The remaining meaningful comparisons should keep rotor loads, collective force, moments, attitude, and motion distinct. A tolerance chosen for floating-point arithmetic is not a physical adequacy threshold. The roadmap retains trajectory/refinement and airflow-cadence failures; a five-second airflow-off generator cannot resolve them [D2]. For temporal approximations, a valid cached root does not establish current geometry/domain admission, and a monitor that reconstructs the full geometry may consume the intended saving [D12]. All monitoring, refresh, rejection, and fallback work must count when a later complete-cost comparison is designed.

Existing records identify substantial antecedents: distributed flocking with obstacle handling, learned aerodynamic residuals, nonlinear aggregate-interaction evidence, the mean-wake law itself, standard directed interaction work, and multirate/prediction methods [D3–D4, D8, D12]. Ordinary far screening or a browser port is therefore not a defensible novelty claim on its own. The literature assessment is focused and incomplete, not proof of absence of prior art. No present source establishes superiority under matched conditions. Reproducible boundary cases and failures can support a computational contribution, but the comparator selection, contribution claim, and adequacy criteria still need explicit justification.

## Suggested measurable next question — planning only

**On the three prospectively fixed five-second states, does unchanged F preserve the full frozen L/F output and control contract and meet the same practical complete-query rule in each of three fresh launches of the named browser?**

If the final design retains six paired measurements per case/process, a concrete conservative decision rule is: both N5000 cases must independently achieve median paired F/L at most 0.90 and at least five strict wins in six comparisons in every declared process, with all numerical, control, ownership, independent-review, and terminal-closure gates passing. This is a recommendation to freeze before execution, not an adopted or executed rule. Keep the N1000 result, every lost pair, process-specific absolute times/ratios, and terminal launch or domain failures in the primary report. Do not pool all repeats into a single rule that can conceal a process failure. No substitution or repeat-after-failure is implied.

A pass would justify retaining F as a candidate for that named browser workload and separately designing integration or stronger computational-baseline work. A failure would delimit portability or practical benefit without undoing the earlier Node result. Either outcome leaves physical validity and novelty unresolved. Before calling a later result an accuracy–computation contribution for the original application, freeze the error reference, downstream observables, acceptable error rationale, matched baselines, and complete browser budget; do not infer them retrospectively from this timing test.

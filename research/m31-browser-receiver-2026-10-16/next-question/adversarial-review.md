# Adversarial static review: next browser wake candidate

2026-10-16. **Recommendation: MODIFY, then prioritize the smaller term-generation plus one serial-reduction question after the receiver's independent saved review closes.** Do not select or launch the candidate as written yet. The four-row slice is suitably bounded; the full R/Q/H ladder, thirty-two scalar controls and two GPU reduction implementations combine more questions than the minimum next experiment needs.

This review used text inspection of the two candidate notes and the independent/M28 source only. No scientific input values, new geometry, terms, sums or roots were evaluated. No numerical module, browser, shader, checker or new agent was run. No repository file was edited. The reported receiver driver pass remains provisional here; it was not independently reproduced.

## What is worth keeping

Four exposed receiving drones with all 5,000 source IDs is a meaningful **enumeration and term-arithmetic slice**, provided the claim stays at sixteen rotor sums. Preparing axes and rotor offsets in binary64 is an honest interface boundary that avoids silently changing the quaternion contract. The saved raw f32 words, original versus represented references, strict domain policy, no omission/grid, no new receiver roots and no timing claims are good choices.

The selection is deliberately biased toward known saved features, not a holdout. “Smallest maximum inflow” can select a completely zero row; call it zero-or-weak unless a positive-only criterion is frozen before selection. A saved contribution count includes profile evaluations whose numerical terms may be zero. Moreover 5,000 source IDs does not mean 5,000 nonzero summands: the 3 m center cutoff and narrow wake support may remove most slots. Report eligible, evaluated and strictly positive term counts separately. A zero row is useful identity coverage, but it is not a weak-positive accumulation stress test. No replacement is warranted if the fixed selection has little positive accumulation depth.

## Main issues before selection

**1. The question promises term preservation, but the candidate gates only total inflow.** Positive and negative *errors* in nonnegative terms can cancel. Passing `abs(S-R)<=epsilon_U` plus exact classifications does not establish that individual terms are accurate. Choose the intended result prospectively:

- For the minimum feasibility claim, say “term-level diagnosis and accuracy of sixteen summed inflows”; retain term errors/L1 totals as diagnostics and do not report a term-accuracy pass.
- If preserving terms is itself required, add a declared term-error criterion. A conservative sufficient choice is `sum_j abs(T_j-R_j) + abs(S-sum_j T_j) <= epsilon_U`, evaluated against an adequately accurate saved-term sum. It charges cancellation rather than benefiting from it. It is a new engineering choice, not an inherited tolerance.

Do not introduce this stronger gate after inspecting the new terms. The analytic receiver sensitivity allocation is valid for the exact positive-parameter law and two nonnegative inflows, but it does not validate a GPU receiver at perturbed U. The candidate correctly declines that composition claim.

**2. R must actually reconstruct the saved I calculation.** The independent source uses its own matrix rotation, `Math.hypot`, left-associated dot expression, factored fade and source ordering. M28 uses double-cross rotation, a reduce-based dot and a subtractive fade. Both describe the intended model, but choosing pieces from each is not a literal reconstruction of the saved I outputs. Freeze R from the independent source and verify its codes and ordinary ascending-source sums against the saved I evidence before proceeding. Keep R's source-induced-speed construction separate from the receiver-only vi constant merely because their mathematical expressions are related.

Implement a selected-receiver reference; do not call the original all-receiver `fields(states)` and filter its output. That would perform an unreserved full field. Global validation/axis preparation may inspect all 5,000 states, but only the four selected receiving rows may evaluate pair terms.

If the shader ports production fade/grouping while Q/H use the independent formulas, its term discrepancy also includes that algebraic implementation choice. Label this honestly; it is not exclusively hardware rounding. The subtractive M28 fade can have different f32 cancellation behavior from the factored reference. A negative result must not trigger a switch to the factored form or a zero clamp within the run.

Freeze all derived-threshold uses. In particular, `down < minDownstream*D` and `down/D < minDownstream` are mathematically related but need not give equivalent f32 decisions. The independent field inlines its profile after the first test; the production helper has a normalized-coordinate domain check. Do not accidentally add a new helper rejection, or reproduce a JavaScript null-to-zero coercion, under an unspecified “same model” label.

**3. H is a conditional diagnostic, not a clean universal geometry/profile split.** At returned `centerNorm/down/radial/axisDot`, H can ask what the host formula would do using those values. H−Q then describes that substituted-geometry calculation; T−H includes threshold construction, normalized coordinates, profile/fade/projection products and their grouping. Neither term uniquely identifies a hardware operation error, especially with fused/reassociated expressions.

Early-return validity is critical. If H takes a different branch and needs a scalar the GPU never computed, there is no H value to reconstruct from the record. For example, a GPU radial-cutoff exit can precede axis-dot evaluation, while H's differently rounded half-width admits the profile and now needs that missing dot. An unreached zero field cannot supply it. Mark that diagnostic unavailable and preserve the branch mismatch; do not recompute geometry from the pose as a hidden H fallback. Freeze the exact stage boundary and required validity bits, and require a complete sufficient record before every H calculation.

H is not necessary for the first decision. R/Q/G already distinguish representation change, branch disagreement and the combined GPU term-arithmetic discrepancy. Defer H for the minimum version, or retain it only as a prospectively charged conditional diagnostic on records with a complete scalar interface. Do not claim a complete H decomposition when only surviving profile records support it.

**4. Separate scalar comparisons do not qualify the composite domain policy.** The thirty threshold triples can demonstrate `<` versus `<=` at explicitly supplied scalar words. They do not test which branch runs first, whether a near-domain exception is hidden by a later zero condition, or whether geometry constructs the threshold correctly. The “all-zero source contribution” control also needs a cause: the fixed model does not have an arbitrary zero-strength source input.

Prefer a small frozen control set that exercises the actual shared decision helper and its precedence: self/outside exclusion, downstream exits, invalid near wake versus near-policy skip, radial cutoff, and a defined valid zero identity. Include at least one complete prepared-geometry near-domain rejection if the experiment is to claim rejection-path qualification. Keep out-of-tilt/projection controls explicitly synthetic. The global tilt rule must have an owner: either host admission checks every uploaded axis before dispatch (and GPU global admission is excluded), or a real whole-input GPU rejection contract must be implemented and budgeted. A per-pair scalar tilt comparison alone is not that contract.

Define separate status values for unwritten output, malformed/nonfinite input, model-domain invalidity and valid zero contribution. Default-zero storage must never be a valid status, even for ordinal zero. For domain-invalid records, clear the numeric-term validity bit; the zero payload is a placeholder, not a summand. Code 7/profile-evaluated with a numerical zero remains valid and distinct from early-zero classifications. State whether fade-region flags are mandatory equality gates or descriptive interval labels; they are not automatically the saved I branch codes.

**5. Prepared f32 axes change the algebraic interface.** For a nonunit decoded axis, `d-(d·axis)axis` is generally not an orthogonal projection. Axis-Z above the tilt cosine is an algebraic predicate, not an independently measured angle unless normalization is also accounted for. Keeping that algebra is acceptable for this explicit approximation test, but do not call the decoded radial value an exact geometric perpendicular distance or the decoded API the original unit-quaternion API. Original/represented norm and margin records are useful evidence, not permission to repair the axis.

## Smallest sound implementation to prioritize

1. Freeze the selected IDs, source/input hashes, original/reference grouping, constants, finite-domain bounds, controls, classification contract and inflow allowance. Keep the four selected rows; reducing four to two saves little complexity and loses useful contrasts.
2. Within the new sole attempt, prepare the 5,000 axes and sixteen offsets once, then compute R and Q for the selected slice. Charge all work and preserve it. Check R against saved I, then stop before GPU work if Q fails the declared admission/classification/representation gate. Selection on saved data is separate from newly evaluating this geometry.
3. One GPU term dispatch writes all 80,000 slot records with typed identity/status, actual term words and the chosen diagnostic scalars. Read back and qualify API, coverage, domains and terms before a dependent reduction. No grid or screening optimization.
4. One serial GPU reduction dispatch reads the **materialized term buffer**, with one invocation per rotor and ascending source order. Compare with the host sum of those exact saved f32 values and with R. Do not merge term production and accumulation into one expression/pass if the promised isolation is “adding the stored terms”: permitted extra precision/fusion could make a running accumulator consume something different from the saved rounded term.
5. Stop with the finite component result. Defer the fixed64 graph and its lane/combine instrumentation. That is a useful later reduction question only after the term interface has qualified. Do not automatically activate it as a reward for a pass.

This means two substantive GPU dispatches, plus a separately frozen small control dispatch if needed; no H pass or parallel reduction is required for the minimum question. R/Q plus returned term data and one serial sum already resolve a significant uncertainty left by the receiver-only test.

## Sums, storage and work accounting

The candidate correctly describes ordinary/Neumaier host sums as numerical references, not exact real sums. That is sufficient if their error is explicitly negligible within a frozen reference allowance. Alternatively, replacing both with one exact integer accumulation of the **stored binary32 terms** is possible because those terms are dyadic rationals; charge and specify that host operation, retain the integer/scaling result, and distinguish it from the unrounded physical terms. Do not call a floating-point Neumaier result exact, and do not import an IEEE error theorem as a WGSL execution guarantee.

If the fixed64 graph is retained despite this recommendation, its label is correctly “strided partial sums plus a balanced combine,” not a fully balanced 5,000-leaf tree. It adds up to 80,000 lane accumulation additions plus 1,008 tree combines across sixteen rotors; the serial graph has 80,000 source accumulation steps when each starts from zero. Its 1,024 lane totals plus 1,008 combine nodes are 2,032 recorded f32 nodes before any separately stored final sums. Declare every lane's source mapping and make all barriers unconditional; inactive terms are not out-of-bounds reads. This extra implementation should have its own numerical conclusion, never a speed implication.

The candidate's conservative R/Q/G reservations reconcile as 239,952 nonself pair/rotor slots, plus up to 79,984 H reconstructions if H is retained, plus sixteen GPU self slots. Those are reservation units, not actual profile evaluations. Distinguish repeated GPU center checks from distinct receiver/source pairs. Geometry preparation, global admission, scalar controls, host saved-term summation and reductions need separate exact counts.

An 80,000×64-byte term record is 5,120,000 bytes. GPU output and its readback consume two such device buffers; that does not imply writing two identical disk copies. Four R/Q/G/H tables, margins and verbose per-mismatch JSON can dominate the proposed evidence cap. Freeze typed strides and a complete byte ledger. Preserve full binary arrays once, then use bounded witness indices/scalars rather than serializing every full state repeatedly. A 64 MiB cap is not an estimate of all host/browser memory. The proposed 60 s ceiling must include initialization, host preparation/reference/attribution, transfers, persistence and owned-process cleanup; it is not a promise of completion.

Do not create prepared axes/offsets or R/Q terms as uncharged “static preparation.” Freeze the rules first; perform and store that new geometry inside the new bounded attempt. No scientific rerun is authorized by a cap, classification or API failure. A saved-only reviewer may reconstruct declared arithmetic from retained outputs, but must not fill missing H fields, regenerate geometry or evaluate another wake.

## Disposition and source snapshot

Prioritize the modified receiver-slice term test only after the current receiver review is resolved and a new protocol is explicitly selected. Keep the candidate unselected until its acceptance claim, composite controls, exact reference implementation and resource ledger are fixed. The fixed64 reduction and complete H attribution are reasonable later questions; including both now increases implementation and evidence obligations without being necessary to decide whether the basic prepared-geometry wake component is viable.

# Closeout privacy and preservation review

The new closeout consists of a saved-result viewer, plain-language milestone, claim ledger, restart note, display QA code/report/screenshots and this review. It introduces no private attachments, conversations, credentials, home-directory paths or runtime installation paths. Existing public evidence contains numeric owned-process IDs, monotonic times, RSS and bounded synthetic error text; the viewer publishes no unrelated process table or command arguments.

A targeted scan of the new textual closeout files checks home/private-temporary path prefixes and common private-key/token patterns. The desktop, mobile and historical scene screenshots were visually inspected and contain only the research page and earlier public drone replay. Repository-relative references, public Git identifiers and localhost8876 are intentional. The scan is bounded and does not claim to recognize every secret format.

The viewer fetches saved records and previously published historical assets. It has no harness, simulator or scientific imports. The six QA corruption cases replace browser responses only, leaving saved files unchanged. Browser QA is separate from both the consumed operational allocation and all earlier scientific allocations.

Before and after closeout, the 16 frozen source-manifest entries, the manifest and publication receipt, the 39 checker evidence pins, and the checker report itself are compared by bytes/SHA256: 58 preserved files. The privacy/preservation check record is saved as `qa/CLOSEOUT-INTEGRITY.json`. This closeout creates no source/result replacement, commit or publication; the parent owns the final manifest and publication.

Caller-attested escalated dispatch is distinguished from what the saved checker actually validates. No root-cause, physical-validity, novelty, speedup or large-swarm claim is added. Historical Three.js flights remain explicitly historical, with airflow off and aerodynamic calculations afterwards.

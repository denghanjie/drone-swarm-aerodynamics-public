# Lossless publication packaging — separate prospective byte operation

The completed native allocation produced four gzip journals totaling 512,819,047 bytes. Two exceed GitHub's regular-file limit. Preserve all original files unchanged locally; do not rerun, recompress, truncate, resample or change any scientific record. This is storage packaging after computation, not a new numerical experiment.

One reviewed byte-only allocation: split each journal into successive chunks of at most 32 MiB in a new exclusive archive/ directory. Input identities come from the unchanged results/index.json. Record each original file's byte count/SHA-256 and each ordered chunk's offset/byte count/SHA-256. Verify the original hash during copying, then independently reread all written chunks in order and require the reconstructed byte count/hash to equal the original journal pin. Internal 180-second limit with 200-second external owned-process supervisor; total output at most 520 MiB. Failure preserves partial output and closes the allocation; no retry. Source/config/native data are unchanged. This operation imports no scientific code and decompresses no numerical record.

For publication exclude only the four original complete gzip files via exact .gitignore paths; publish every chunk, the mapping and verification receipt, and the original results/index.json. Final manifest pins the published chunks rather than pretending unpublished full gzip blobs exist in Git. A reader reconstructs each original by concatenating its listed parts, verifies its original SHA-256, and can then inspect the unchanged journal. Do not interpret successful packaging as new scientific evidence.

The earlier 512 MiB native cap applies to native results. This separate archival budget covers the additional retained byte copy. Files are synthetic computational research evidence; no personal attachments or credentials are introduced. Privacy scanning and independent final result review still precede publication. The operation requires reviewed source and a frozen archive-source manifest, public push and exact-public verification before invocation.

# Similar at the center, different across the profile

**The two published normalized wake profiles have nearly equal center speeds, but a 24.1% discrepancy under our fixed whole-profile metric.** This is a descriptive result from published plotted medians, not a new physical experiment or a statistically established failure of the paper's model.

Think of checking a hill by measuring only its height. Two hills can have nearly the same height and different widths. Here, a center-speed check similarly misses differences away from the wake center. At three motor distances below the drones, the normalized centerline medians are 1.155 and 1.157. The radial locations where each falls to half its own center value are 0.449 and 0.559 motor distances. The largest normalized gap is 0.258 at radius 0.381 motor distances.

We extracted the orange/yellow median curves in [Bauersfeld et al., Fig. 7, version 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13321v3), covering only the displayed radial interval. These two conditions have the same tabulated frame dimensions and propeller diameter, with masses 0.572 and 1.207 kg. We left the curves unchanged in the paper's physical normalization: no fitted amplitude, width, peak alignment or target-area normalization. The fitted black line was excluded.

The 24.1% value is the radially weighted relative L2 discrepancy using Offboard 2 as reference. It summarizes profile differences while giving outer radii greater weight. It is not an average percentage error at every point, thrust error or a universal accuracy rating. The source/protocol was [published before calculating this contrast](https://github.com/denghanjie/drone-swarm-aerodynamics-public/commit/37750e0ea4d929ac7bb414a7636cfebaea3f3c2a). Independent PDF parsers agree on the visible coordinates; a separate reviewer reproduced the arithmetic using different quadrature and passed 38 checks.

**What improved:** we now have an auditable spatial comparison to observed, processed hover data rather than only model-to-model consistency. It demonstrates why our future accuracy measurements should cover a spatial profile, not just a center value.

**What remains unresolved:** the original samples and uncertainty of these median curves are unavailable. We cannot judge statistical significance, isolate weight as the cause, validate a motor-command law, or determine whether the discrepancy is acceptable for a particular drone-control task. The paper already contributed to the current model, so this is exposed-data reanalysis, not held-out validation. We have not established novelty or 5,000-drone feasibility.

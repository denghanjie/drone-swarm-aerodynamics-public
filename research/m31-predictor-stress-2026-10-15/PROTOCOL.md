# Prospective predictor stress challenge

Unexecuted until reviewed, frozen and pushed. The previous restricted envelope challenge (`f569093` source; `d82a087` results) found a two-sample causal inflow predictor more accurate and faster than its adaptive envelope on one exposed path. This follow-up asks whether that lead survives six fresh, explicitly declared model-input stresses. No parameters will be tuned against these outcomes and no failed run retried.

The broad algorithm is known prior art: Grubmüller and Tavan (1998), DC-1b, explicitly estimates forces from the two latest exact samples. Here it is used for inflow followed by the nonlinear current-pose receiver response. See [verified five-paper prior-art note](literature-and-prior-work.md), especially T1's equation/table, and the existing [aerodynamic evidence ledger](../m31-restart-2026-10-15/literature/annotated-ledger.md). No novelty is presumed, and the bounded search is not exhaustive.

## Inputs and assumptions

Use exactly the six analytic cases and static domain argument in `DESIGN-REVIEW.md`, implemented by `source/cases.cjs`. Durations are one second each. They test support crossing; cold start already inside a wake; smooth reversal; changing source pitch; changing receiver roll beneath two sources; and a moving receiver with three sources, two moving. All orientations and nominal motor thrusts are prescribed, not obtained from equations of flight. Body velocities/angular rates follow the analytic input where supplied; the M28 field does not consume them. Nominal rotor thrust is mg/4 throughout. M28 source strength depends on isolated hover weight, not source motor states, so varying source thrust would not test source-response physics in this model.

The unchanged `sum-rotor` field is a quasi-static far-field/mean-wake extension: it lacks transport delay, wake history, walls, ground effect, translational airspeed and measured unsteady coupling. The original measured-source scale starts at230g while this model vehicle is27g. The independent oracle is an equation-implementation check, not physical validation. The suite is deliberately selected and finite, not a random sample of flight environments. Center separations and tilt bounds must exclude the model's near-field/attitude domain errors for all ordered vehicle pairs, including source-to-source interactions. The preflight reviewer must check this analytically before execution.

## Matched comparisons and prospective outcomes

For every case compare instantaneous field; holds10/5/2.5ms; and predict5ms. All begin with an actual field evaluation at t=0. Prediction holds until its second real sample. Thereafter each rotor inflow is max(0, u_k + (t-t_k)/(t_k-t_(k-1))*(u_k-u_(k-1))). Only actual field samples enter history. Hold5ms and predict5ms have identical refresh times and field-call counts. Current receiver rotor loads are recomputed at every query; only inflow is cached or predicted. Predicting force directly is outside this fixed suite.

Save every fine-grid pose, all-vehicle production and independent reference winds, independent receiver0 loads, field candidate/contribution counts, receiver0 treatment winds/loads, and refresh-left values. Report each case separately: augmented peak lift/roll/pitch error; event-split time RMS; peak location; counts of sampled target exceedances; minimum/maximum reference inflow; independent arithmetic discrepancies; complete query-loop timings and call counts. No case or bad metric is dropped. Report signed treatment-minus-reference load and inflow extrema in the two predeclared startup windows (0–5ms and5–10ms) and ±10ms around each of the three interior reversals; include both sides at boundaries. These are descriptive windows, not extra refreshes. Thresholds remain0.005mg lift and0.005mgD each moment; these are numerical targets with no safety significance. Exceedances are findings, not grounds for tuning or aborting a correctly executed experiment.

Compare primary0.5ms and refined0.25ms queries on identical refresh schedules. Include all update-left values in peaks, including terminal t=1s. At refreshes, old inflow terminates the preceding squared-error trapezoid and the new value begins the next. Require differences ≤max(5% refined metric,1e-9N or1e-11N·m) for each peak/RMS screen. Preserve failures; no further refinement. The earlier uniform-RMS failures remain failed under their original estimand. Counts of exceedance samples are grid-dependent, not failure probabilities.

A suite-level claim that predict5ms improves all tested peak errors over hold5ms requires all18 case×observable comparisons to satisfy predictorPeak≤holdPeak+floor (floor1e-9N thrust or1e-11N·m torque), at least one strict predictorPeak<holdPeak−floor, and both methods' peak sampling screens to pass for all18 comparisons. RMS is assessed separately by the same rule using RMS values and both RMS screens. Zero/small values are included; no undefined "nontrivial" subset is selected. Never substitute an average win for a failed case. Passing targets in a case requires all three refined peaks within targets plus the1e-12 arithmetic allowance and that method's three peak sampling screens. If outcomes are mixed, identify the input mechanism and propose a new separately frozen experiment; do not silently add a limiter/reset. Universal/flight-safety claims are out of scope.

Stored observables are body-axis total rotor thrust T and body roll/pitch torque. Under receiver roll, thrust is not world-vertical lift. The cached/predicted scalar inflow already includes receiver-axis projection from its refresh pose; there is no additional current-attitude projection outside the field call. The receiver law recomputes thrust from current nominal motor states and the supplied scalar inflow, then sums body moments. It does not add unmodeled attitude or relative-velocity physics.

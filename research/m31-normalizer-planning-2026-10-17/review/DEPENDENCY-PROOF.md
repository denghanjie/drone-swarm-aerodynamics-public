**2. Actual API and ownership contract.** The draft exports legacy `query(receiver,source,rotor)` without caching and `createQuery()`, whose returned three-argument query closes over two private WeakMaps. The factory must be called once for each complete method invocation. No cache, cached interval or factory closure is returned in the method record or stored at module scope. In draft methods line 27, creation occurs after `onPhase('spatial')` and before scan; both exact fallback sites use this same closure. Every method call constructs a new exact Map and fresh exact prepared objects, even when IDs repeat. Invalid global preparation returns before factory creation. A valid one-drone/zero-query invocation still pays the factory allocation.

This is a safe *closed-method* API under these preconditions: exact prepared objects and their force rationals remain unchanged while the closure is used; arithmetic modules, captured constants, builtins and their relevant prototypes remain fixed; inputs are ordinary data rather than proxies/getters; and allocations complete normally. The returned closure must not be retained and reused for a later state. The implementation does not enforce these preconditions for arbitrary callers of the exported factory. It must not be advertised as safe for mutation between calls. Adding freezing or new validation would be a separate behavior change, not part of this draft.

**3. What is and is not immutable.** `interval.cjs` uses `SHIFT=96n`, `S=2^96`, `MAX=2^352`; its interval constructor validates and returns `Object.freeze({l,u})`. Arithmetic returns newly constructed frozen intervals. BigInt endpoints are immutable primitives. `I.encode` creates fresh string-endpoint records, so a pair result does not expose the cached interval for mutation. Successful interval sharing is therefore safe.

The same physical-freezing claim is false for the rational inputs. `rational.cjs` constructs ordinary `{n,d}` records; exported `zero`/`one` are ordinary mutable objects. Its arithmetic reads operands without assigning to their fields, and `sum` reduces in array order. `adapter.cjs` exports an ordinary mutable `exact` object. Pair code's `const C=A.exact` is an alias, not a freeze or value snapshot. The method's exact Map, arrays and rational records are also not frozen. Their stability follows from ownership and the inspected call graph, under the contract above.

The inspected non-mutation closure is concrete:

* `methods.cjs` creates exact force rationals from admitted word strings. They remain internal and are not part of the returned `prepared` word records. No assembly code assigns to their fields or arrays.
* `pair-prepared.cjs`, `rational.cjs` and `interval.cjs` read force/constants and construct results. The draft writes only private maps after successful normalizer calculation.
* `tree.cjs` reads the exact Map, sorts its own ID arrays, and builds nodes/permutation. Its rule counters do not modify exact objects. `boxes.cjs` sometimes aliases rational endpoints through min/max/point, but does not mutate them.
* `zero-filter.cjs` prepares separate fast geometry from word strings. `binary64-interval.cjs` has private DataView scratch and no access to the exact Map or `A.exact`; its endpoint operations do not mutate exact force objects.
* `loads.cjs` runs after pair assembly, consuming prepared words and encoded coefficients. It constructs its own arithmetic objects and has no access to the cache or method-local exact Map.

All phase callbacks are synchronous calls outside query assembly: `prepared`, then `spatial`, then `assembled` after the last scalar query. There is no await, yield or user callback in the reviewed assembly loop. Earlier callbacks can in principle alter exported globals, and a general caller can supply mutable raw input. The conditional proof requires immutable invocation inputs and no relevant mutation or monkeypatching during cache lifetime; it does not infer enforcement from the callback names. The existing `makeInput` freezes its raw input structure. Timing-dependent external mutation or instrumentation is outside the theorem. The assembled callback occurs after the cache's final use.

**4. Lazy expression and exception proof.** The baseline evaluates center/rotor/down/radial/width/projection guards, then enters the try block, computes profile, radial square root and the complete faded `K`, then beta, then nu, then scaled K and the cap tests, then `nu.l<=0`, then the coefficient. In particular, both normalizers precede cap refusal. The draft preserves that order (pair lines 19–32), including the separate beta and nu evaluation sites. No normalizer runs on an earlier zero/rejected path. K failures retain priority over normalizer failures; beta failures retain priority over nu failures. Cap checks cannot be moved earlier merely because they might reject the pair.

A cache miss computes the identical expression and stores only after it returns (pair lines 40–50). An exception leaves that key absent. It propagates back into the original try/catch; `I.Unqualified` still becomes unresolved with the same `error.code`, while other errors still escape. A hit returns the same frozen interval endpoints as recomputation because dependencies and arithmetic remain fixed. Failed expressions are attempted again on every later reaching query. A successful beta may be retained when nu subsequently fails. That changes only repeated pure beta work, not the failure that determines the pair result.

Here “successful” means that the normalizer expression returned an interval. It does not mean that the pair became positive or that nu has a strictly positive lower bound. An interval with `nu.l===0n` is still a valid cached value; the original per-pair normalizer refusal remains. Successful intervals may also be retained after cap rejection/unresolved status. No branch, exception, cap result, coefficient, load, solve or certificate is cached.

An induction over the actual scalar-query sequence gives the conditional record proof. Initially caches are empty and the scientific state is the baseline state. For each query, unchanged guards/K reach the same next site or return the same earlier result. At each reached normalizer site, a miss executes the identical expression, and a hit supplies identical frozen endpoints. The original catch and all later comparisons/coefficient encoding therefore produce identical scientific data. Cache writes do not modify subsequent dependencies. Traversal, filter and tree decisions, query order, counters, pair sorting and refusal priorities remain unchanged. Consequently the unchanged load/solve/certificate path receives identical inputs and returns the same complete scientific record, assuming the same relevant callback effects and ordinary successful allocations.

This is equality of scientific data and statuses, not of object identity, function introspection, call stacks, allocation count, elapsed time, GC behavior or resource termination. New WeakMap/Map allocation can fail before a query or after a calculation; out-of-memory and externally imposed timeouts need not occur at the same point. Extra call frames can change stack traces. These limits prevent an unconditional claim of all observable JavaScript behavior being identical.

**5. Exact count and memory bound.** Let `N` be the admitted drone count and `Q` the number of exact scalar queries actually invoked. The reviewed traversal bounds `Q <= 4N(N-1)`. Let `B` be the number reaching beta after K, and `V` the number reaching nu after beta returns, so `V <= B <= Q`. Let `U_beta` and `U_nu` count distinct successful source and receiver/rotor keys, and let `F_beta` and `F_nu` count failed expression attempts, including retries.

Candidate beta attempts are exactly `U_beta + F_beta`; candidate nu attempts are exactly `U_nu + F_nu`. Since `U_beta <= N` and `U_nu <= 4N`, total normalizer attempts are at most `5N + F_beta + F_nu`, and also at most `B + V <= 2Q`. Relative to baseline, the count saved is `(B - F_beta - U_beta) + (V - F_nu - U_nu)`. When every reached expression succeeds, at most `5N` normalizer square-root evaluations replace `B+V` evaluations. There is no linear bound on failures: repeated failed keys can still require quadratic work. Radial square root, K, geometry and downstream work are unchanged, so this is not a linear bound on the complete method.

Storage is at most `N` beta entries, `4N` nu entries and `N` inner Maps, plus two WeakMaps and the closure: O(N) cached interval storage. The inherited `N<=180` limit implies at most 900 successful cached interval entries; this is a static bound, not an observation. WeakMap does not imply immediate reclamation, zero GC cost or a performance improvement. Cache allocation and lookups can outweigh arithmetic savings on sparse/zero cases. Only a separately authorized complete-cost experiment can establish benefit.

**6. Prospective adversarial witnesses, specified but not executed.** A future fixed protocol should distinguish these cases without reopening historical evidence:

* Repeated active pairs sharing a source across receivers/rotors; repeated receiver/rotor across sources; distinct receiver rotor forces. These challenge both key dimensions and ensure reuse does not cross rotor indices.
* Successive method calls with identical IDs and changed force words; same source total with redistributed rotor forces. Fresh invocation ownership prevents stale reuse, while nu remains rotor specific.
* Tiny admitted positive receiver force whose directed conversion has zero lower bound. A returned nu interval must remain cacheable while every applicable pair still returns the same normalizer refusal.
* Large admitted finite positive forces whose ratio exceeds the interval range. The represented-value endpoint limit is `MAX/S = 2^256`; use symbolically chosen operands satisfying the range condition and qualify exact expected outcomes prospectively. Raw positive-force admission does not itself impose this magnitude bound. Repeated `I.Unqualified` failures must remain uncached.
* Early geometry zero/rejection with pathological force; profile/fade/K failure before either normalizer; both normalizers capable of failing; beta success followed by nu failure. These challenge laziness, priority, retention of beta and retry of nu.
* Successful beta/nu followed by cap rejection or unresolved cap; successful nu with zero lower bound. Reuse must not cache the later branch or skip per-pair checks.
* Zero/negative or nonfinite forces causing global preparation refusal before the factory. No successful normalizer entries should be inferred from such invocations.
* Ordinary rational errors, malformed standalone objects and injected non-I exceptions are useful isolated control-flow specifications, but fall outside ordinary admitted immutable inputs. They must not be silently converted to `I.Unqualified`. Such injection would require its own authorization and source package.
* An explicit unsupported counterexample is calling exported `createQuery` twice with the same object identity after modifying a force rational or `A.exact` constant: a prior successful entry can become stale. This demonstrates the API boundary; the current draft does not solve arbitrary mutable-client reuse.

No diagnostic counter was added or evaluated here. Any future instrumentation must be separately specified so that it does not change the full-record equality target. Before any experiment, freeze and verify the complete dependency package, define fresh moving seeds/schedule and bounded paired complete-cost checks, and obtain independent saved-evidence checking under the prospective allocation.

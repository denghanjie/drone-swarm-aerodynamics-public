# Independent claims and application-target challenge

This is a source-only review of the exact pins in `CLAIMS-READING.json`. It authorizes no import, scientific execution, control evaluation, benchmark, profiling, retrieval or new flight. Twelve inherited files were read; the large inherited `spec.json` was only partially displayed, with its first 85 lines subsequently read explicitly. Current-package drafts are inventoried separately. No old source, result or failure was changed.

## Decision

Preserve the minimal beta/nu reuse draft as a plausible, narrow optimization candidate, conditional on the closed-call immutability proof. This is standard memoization with unknown complete-cost benefit. It is a useful next source-design step because the operands repeat by construction and the proposed change can be isolated without changing numerical tolerance. It is not yet evidence that square roots, rational arithmetic or normalizers are the bottleneck. It is not a demonstrated route to real-time 5,000-drone operation.

The inherited method explicitly rejects input sizes above `spec.maxDrones = 180`; its positive-term cap is 2,000,000. The draft preserves both restrictions. It therefore cannot admit 5,000 drones at all under the current contract. Increasing either bound is a separate source/protocol/resource decision, not a harmless consequence of reuse. Per-interaction geometry, profile arithmetic, radial square root, coefficient work, traversal, load solving and certification remain. The complete logical partition is still `4*N*(N-1)`, even where the hierarchy accounts for many interactions in groups. Reusing at most N successful beta values and 4N successful nu values reduces this repeated subwork; it does not prove linear total cost or eliminate dense interaction growth.

The strongest reason to defer this candidate would be an application requirement dominated by model refusal, physical prediction, browser portability or temporal coupling. Those are unresolved. Within the narrower aim of lowering the cost of the current exact oracle while preserving its semantics, this candidate is sufficiently small to justify completing its proof and then deciding whether to allocate a bounded paired test. A standalone fresh admission replication would test generalization but leave the cost question untouched; fresh moving-state admission should be an independent gate inside any later paired test. Neither test is allocated here.

## What the inspected source supports

`pair-prepared.cjs` computes beta from `sqrt(sum(source.forces)/mg)` and nu from `sqrt(receiver.forces[rotor]/(2*rho*A))`. Both are reached only after the same ordered geometric guards, profile construction, radial square root and K construction. They execute beta first, then nu, before the cap and positive-normalizer decisions. Consequently a pair need not finish positive for a successfully computed normalizer to be reusable.

The arithmetic graph and operand order matter. The intended source-beta key is the exact source object belonging to this method invocation. The receiver-nu key is the exact receiver object plus its rotor index. The fixed constant environment is an implicit part of both keys and must be invariant while the cache is live. Stable drone ID alone, array position alone, nominally similar force values, geometry alone, or a frame counter are not adequate substitutes for that lifetime/dependency proof.

`interval.cjs` creates frozen endpoint objects, and its square root is a directed integer construction over fixed-point endpoints. `rational.cjs` operations inspected here read operands and return values without assigning into input rationals. However, the rational objects, the exact prepared objects and exported `adapter.exact` are not deeply frozen. Immutability is a property to prove for the closed synchronous call graph, not an existing JavaScript type guarantee. This review did not inspect `tree.cjs` or every caller, so it does not independently close that whole-graph obligation. The source reviewer must establish that tree operations, callbacks and module consumers cannot modify force operands, relevant constants or arithmetic functions during assembly. An arbitrary caller retaining an exported `createQuery()` while mutating its arguments is outside this safe-use claim.

The draft creates one closure after `onPhase('spatial')` and before scans, with no callback inside the displayed scan loop. Its exact objects are constructed anew per method call. The two WeakMaps are private; the nu map contains a rotor-keyed Map per encountered receiver. A hit returns the frozen successful interval. A miss performs the identical expression and stores only after success. This preserves the original beta-before-nu order and `I.Unqualified` catch site. Ordinary programming errors continue to propagate. Error-stack identity, out-of-memory behavior and runtime scheduling are not claimed identical; resource behavior is part of later measurement and containment.

Cache creation is paid even if all subsequent interactions are skipped. No geometry, center-distance calculation, coefficient, cap result, branch, solver result or certificate is cached. The existing pair/filter counters remain the same because exact-query reach and traversal remain the same. They cannot serve as a measurement of normalizer work saved. Candidate identity must be carried by the future source/runner binding: its method label remains `filtered`, so that label alone cannot distinguish arms.

The inspected draft is acceptable as the minimal candidate. Retain the uncached query interface for the baseline and independent checks; create a fresh cache for every method invocation; preserve successful values only; discard all references on return. Avoid adding eager precomputation, cross-frame reuse, a new denominator simplification, center-geometry reuse or another arithmetic change to the same ablation. A later claim of benefit would apply to the combined beta/nu memoization change unless separately allocated ablations distinguish them.

## What the cost evidence does not establish

The inherited filter-cost milestone reports a dense-180 filtered median of 3.891 seconds, with combined assembly averaging 3.775 seconds of a 3.923-second complete method. It reports 24/24 paired wins for the earlier filtering change. Those figures concern fixed static groups and a different computational change. They are motivation to reduce assembly cost, not measurements of beta, nu, integer square roots, rational reductions, cache lookup overhead or moving-state cost.

No fraction of time attributable to normalizers has been measured in this allocation. An Amdahl-style upper bound would require that fraction; it cannot be inferred from combined assembly timing. Even a large reduction in normalizer evaluation count might yield little end-to-end benefit if geometric arithmetic dominates. Sparse/all-zero states can pay the closure cost without gaining a hit. Small or weakly reused states may pay more lookup/allocation cost than they save. Deferred garbage collection can shift cost to the next call or beyond the timing boundary. These are live competing explanations, not reasons to presume success or failure.

## Proposed application target: assumptions, not established requirements

The following is a concrete discussion target proposed by this reviewer. None of its hardware, cadence, density, tail-latency or accuracy choices was established by the user, measured here, or added to an execution protocol. It is intentionally explicit so a later decision can accept or replace it without quietly redefining success.

| Dimension | Proposed target and boundary |
|---|---|
| Reference platform | One Apple M2 desktop/laptop with 8 CPU cores and 16 GiB RAM, plugged in, with exact OS/browser build and power policy fixed in the future protocol. This is a hypothetical reference machine, not an identification of the present host. Single browser worker for the evaluated method; main-thread rendering measured separately. |
| Population and motion | 5,000 moving drones in a fixed, independently validated 3D state schedule. Every scheduled state counts. Start from a declared finite 10-second observation horizon; select repetition and warmup lengths prospectively rather than from observed tails. No reseeding, dropping refused states or substituting an easier schedule. |
| Local support density | For each scheduled state, count other centers within the unchanged 3 m neighbor support: mean at most 32, 95th percentile at most 64, maximum at most 128 per receiver. Separately record actual positive source terms per receiver rotor: mean at most 8, 95th percentile at most 16, maximum at most 32. These are assumed workload bounds, not consequences of N or Boids. Publish the full distributions and minima/nonzero fractions as well. |
| Density violations | Retain every violation as an out-of-target or failed scheduled state. Do not prune real model terms to enforce the target. Fix additional dense/stress contexts separately and report them without pooling them into the primary density class. |
| Fresh-load stream | 20 updates per second. Proposed complete-method latency: p95 at most 25 ms and p99 at most 50 ms; proposed capture-to-available-result latency including worker transfer/queueing: p99 at most 50 ms. Report maximum, misses, backlog and denominator too. These budgets are engineering assumptions; a percentile alone is not a hard real-time guarantee. |
| Presentation | 60 Hz target, main-thread frame p95 at most 16.7 ms, with state/result ages shown. UI smoothness must not conceal stale loads, dropped model queries or missed worker deadlines. Rendering evidence cannot establish load correctness. |
| Numerical accuracy | Preserve exact baseline scientific records for the isolated candidate, including branch/refusal/witness and encoded intervals. Independently certify all four existing normalized load-error bounds at most 1e-8 on admitted states, using the unchanged normalization and reference contract. Report admission/certification against all scheduled states, separately from conditional accuracy among admitted states. |
| Physical accuracy | No physical-error threshold is established by these calculations. A future physical claim needs independent measurements, a declared force/torque/flight metric, units, uncertainty and operating envelope. The 1e-8 numerical bound is not an aerodynamic prediction error bound. |

This first target describes a fresh-load diagnostic/oracle stream on moving states. It does not yet describe a coupled 5,000-drone flight simulator. The inherited generator uses `dt = 0.0025` seconds, airflow off, and posthoc load evaluations; 51 displayed poses per run and scheduled 0/5/10-second load states are different records. Applying a 20 Hz load stream to a 400 Hz integrator would introduce held or interpolated loads, or another scheduling policy. Before claiming coupled flight, a separate protocol must fix the integration and aerodynamic update/substage cadence, pay all such evaluations, test feedback and temporal convergence, and preserve refusals throughout. No reduced aerodynamic cadence or temporal-accuracy allowance is part of this normalizer candidate.

The proposed density class and hardware must be fixed before a 5,000-drone performance claim. A finite small-N gain cannot be extrapolated to these budgets. A model that refuses an important moving state has not met this application target merely because it refuses quickly. If the actual requirement is physical prediction rather than a numerically faithful oracle, calibration and model adequacy take priority over this optimization for that requirement.

## Evidence required before the next decision

1. **Source proof and frozen identity.** Resolve the complete closed-call nonmutation and callback premises; bind the entire baseline/candidate dependency closures, runtime, contract and fixed protocol; independently verify public source before any execution. The current draft alone does not constitute that gate. Preserve all old failed allocations as failures.
2. **Dependency and exception controls.** Prospectively include distinct source force patterns, unequal receiver rotor forces, shuffled stable IDs/order, repeated calls with the same IDs but changed force/state words, source/receiver-role swaps, and different rotor indices. Include zero/refused paths that stop before either normalizer; beta failure; beta success followed by nu failure; successful normalizers followed by cap/normalizer refusal; and repeated successful values after such a refusal. For cases requiring crafted operands, state whether they are supported method cases or isolated query controls. Do not pretend a control outside the admitted domain is application evidence.
3. **Independent numerical checking.** Compare complete method records exactly and independently reconstruct the unchanged model results and all load bounds. Where raw beta/nu outputs are checked, validate them separately: two wrong normalizers can partly compensate in a coefficient ratio. Checker controls must fail for the intended reason, including cache-lifetime/rotor/dependency corruption, and must reach that reason rather than an earlier unrelated guard. Reference uncertainty, model refusal and checker failure remain distinct outcomes.
4. **Fresh moving admission.** Predeclare new seeds and all scheduled states and nontriviality tests in a future protocol. Preserve missing/rejected/unresolved states in denominators; no reseeding or selective retention. A fixed replay of the already exposed states is useful regression evidence, not fresh replication. Admission on isolated snapshots does not establish admission between them or at integration substages.
5. **Complete paired cost.** Same immutable raw input per pair; fixed balanced order and bounded repetitions; source-bound arms; full admission, preparation, hierarchy, filter, exact work, solver, certificate, closure creation, lookups, misses and return inside the method boundary. Declare input conversion, serialization, reference checking and worker transfer separately, and include them where the application boundary requires them. Account for allocation, retained memory, cleanup and garbage-collection spillover across a cadence stream; do not reward moving cleanup outside a short timing window.
6. **Prospective decision rule.** Fix a practically meaningful improvement criterion, per-class failure/timeout treatment and caps before results. Report all pairs, tails and absolute times as well as ratios. Any rule such as a 10% median complete-cost reduction is only a candidate rule until frozen in that protocol. Operation-count reductions cannot replace a complete-cost result. Passing a small-N ablation would justify that limited optimization claim, not the proposed application target.

## Attribution and permitted claim

The inherited literature record describes Shewchuk (1997), Brönnimann/Burnikel/Pion (2001), and Melquiond/Pion (2007) for adaptive exact fallback, interval inclusion and arithmetic guards. This review read that record, not the full papers anew, and performed no new retrieval. Those papers do not prove this cache correct or fast and do not validate the aerodynamic model. Within-call reuse of a pure repeated expression is ordinary memoization/common-subexpression reuse. No priority or algorithmic novelty claim is supported.

The strongest present claim is: **a source-only draft isolates successful within-invocation beta/nu reuse with explicit dependency, lifetime and exception obligations; no measured benefit, new admission, physical validity, browser transfer or 5,000-drone capability follows.** The draft may advance to completion of its source proof and protocol design. It may not advance directly to numerical execution from this review.

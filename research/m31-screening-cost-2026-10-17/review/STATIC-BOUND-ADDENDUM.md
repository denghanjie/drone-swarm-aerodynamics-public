## Active-support count

Require all quaternions to be exactly the identity, the frozen corners to have zero z coordinate, and every position literal to be the declared offset plus an integer grid index times 0.43 or 3.4, rounded once to binary64. The inherited rotation graph then gives axis `(0,0,1)`, offsets `(±arm,±arm,0)` and prepared point z equal to center z. Therefore exact down-distance is source z minus receiver z, and the exact residual is horizontal.

The constant graph gives `spread < 0.077`, `-origin < 6`, `D < 0.08`, `radCut = 3`, and `downCut = 2.5`. These are coarse strict source bounds: decimal-literal rounding is far smaller than their margins; `sqrt(2)` lies strictly between 1.414 and 1.415, and the rounded `R = sqrt(2)*arm` remains below 0.04, hence `D = 2R < 0.08`. The independently regenerated constant encodings must match the candidate as usual.

Any active pair has `0 < h < 2.5` and

`width = spread*(h-origin*D) < 0.077*(2.5+6*0.08) = 0.22946`.

Consequently its radial distance is strictly below `3*width < 0.68838 < 0.689`. Dense literal center coordinates have absolute value below 3, so each correctly rounded literal has error at most `2^-52`. A two-index coordinate separation is therefore greater than `0.86-2^-51 > 0.859999999`.

The relevant rotor bound is **the actual prepared difference `p-c`**, including the rounding of `c+offset`. Bounding the stored offset alone would not suffice. The stored arm is below 0.028000001, and the much smaller final addition error leaves `|p_x-c_x|` and `|p_y-c_y|` below 0.028000001 with ample margin. Thus a source separated by two dense grid indices in x or y has a residual coordinate above `0.859999999-0.028000001 = 0.831999998`, exceeding the active support radius. At most nine horizontal columns can contribute to a receiver rotor, with at most `edge-1` strictly higher source levels.

For the three dense scenes this gives the conservative bound

`4*64*9*3 + 4*125*9*4 + 4*216*9*5 = 63,792`.

Their center magnitude guards are safely within the declared finite lattice extent. Subject to valid preparation they are neighbor zeros, so they add no active entries.

The current `adapter.cjs`, `pair-prepared.cjs` and `tree.cjs` source also establish the early-branch conditions needed to bound **all serialized nonzero entries**, including rejected/unresolved outcomes. ## Dyadic sizes and 256-character rationals

Each 54-step native bisection begins with endpoints 0 and 1. Inductively, after step k both endpoints lie on the `2^-k` grid. Binary64 rounding can coarsen that grid, not introduce a finer one; division by two is exact here and the values remain normal. The final midpoint therefore has denominator at most `2^55`, independently of the proposal's accuracy. The independent 128-step exact bisection gives reference endpoints with denominator at most `2^128`.

The four frozen motor literals lie between `2^-5` and `2^-3`, hence their binary64 denominators are at most `2^57`. The arm lies between `2^-6` and `2^-5`, hence its denominator is at most `2^58`. Identity-axis levers are just signed arm coordinates. Candidate rotor/collective/world-force values therefore have denominators at most `2^112`, and candidate moments at most `2^170`. Reference rotor/force values have denominators at most `2^185`, and reference moments at most `2^243`. Addition of dyadics does not multiply these denominator bounds. The named load magnitudes are below one, so their reduced numerator magnitudes obey the same power-of-two bound. Even `2^243` has only 74 decimal digits; sign overhead leaves ample room within 256 characters.

The strict rotor-error control witness is a force times a candidate/reference root gap divided by `mg/4`. The adapter computes `mg = RN(RN(.027)*RN(9.81))`. Coarse strict literal bounds place it in `(1/4,1/2)`, so write `mg=M/2^54` with integer `2^52 < M < 2^53`. Write a force as `F/2^57`, with `0 < F < 2^54`, and the root distance as `G/2^128`, with `0 <= G <= 2^128`. Then the normalized witness has the unreduced representation `F*G*2^56 / (2^185*M)`. Both integers are below `2^238`, at most 72 decimal digits. Reduction can only shorten them. This explicitly bounds the mg numerator as well as its denominator.

A quantized fixed-192 coefficient witness also fits. For an independently positive result, scaled-K upper is at most 128 and the positive fixed-192 normalizer lower is at least `2^-192`; the coefficient upper is therefore at most `2^199`. Outward fixed-192 encoding gives numerator at most `2^391` and denominator at most `2^192`, still below 256 decimal characters. This bound is deliberately coarse and needs no coefficient evaluation.

The concrete `RESULT-CONTRACT-DRAFT.json` now enumerates emitted fraction sites. The additional sites fit the same cap: tree center endpoints are frozen raw coordinates with absolute value below 18 and nonzero magnitude above 1/8, hence denominator at most `2^55` and numerator below `2^60`; axis endpoints are 0 or 1. A native candidate midpoint is at least `2^-55`. Toggling its least-significant binary64 bit while requiring the result in `(0,1]` can refine its denominator only to `2^107`; multiplying by a force gives at most `2^164`. Adding 1 to a candidate rotor load keeps its denominator at most `2^112` and raises magnitude only below 2. Root/load-box witnesses are subsets of the previously bounded reference intervals. Timing/work fractions use at most 20-digit timestamp operands and fixed finite counts, leaving even an unreduced ratio of medians below 42 decimal digits per integer. These bounds cover all ten enumerated fraction categories at this contract snapshot. Do not emit unquantized intermediate oracle fractions or a new fraction site under that proof without separate prospective bounds. Enforce every cap with explicit failure, never truncation or rounding.

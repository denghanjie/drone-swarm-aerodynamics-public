How the comparison was controlled

Both rules use identical vehicle mechanics, hardware limits, initial positions, batch capacities and 60-second horizons. The new rule changes timing and staging thresholds; it does not increase maximum requested speed or relax the 20 cm desired separation or 15 cm contact envelope. It still uses a central scheduler with exact state information.

The study compares 180 starting conditions: 120 from the previous milestone plus 60 additional cases using seeds 11–15. The new settings were fixed after seed-1 pilot runs, before evaluating the additional seeds. These seeds vary initial lateral position and altitude by only 5 mm, so this is not a test of sensor noise, delayed communication or real-flight uncertainty.

The three replays use seed 1, 0.6 m initial spacing and 0.5 m/s requested speed. All vehicles fly near the same height. This is 3D rigid-body motion and visualization, not yet a volumetric swarm with multiple planned heights. The opening is a virtual measurement plane; impact and wall dynamics are not modeled.

Physics is calculated on the CPU, then replayed with Three.js/WebGL. No aerodynamic interaction, wind, sensor error or delay is included. Physical accuracy, real-flight safety, optimality and novelty remain unestablished.

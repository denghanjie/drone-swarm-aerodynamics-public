# Milestone 30 — wall-priority repair

Question: does preserving obstacle-clearance requests after local pair avoidance prevent the retained large-flock wall failures, and what happens on untouched starting seeds?

The baseline is the frozen milestone 26 six-pass sequential filter. The candidate keeps the same Boids, shared route, neighbor sensing, initial states, body dynamics, control interface and geometry. It takes the baseline velocity and uses Dykstra's alternating projections with correction vectors to project onto the intersection of the existing obstacle halfspaces and the 0.9 m/s speed ball. No new clearance margin or gains are tuned. Each correction solves a small convex command-space problem; it is not a dynamic safety certificate. The finite solver stops at residual <= 1e-9 m/s and iteration displacement <= 1e-10 m/s, or 512 iterations. Nonconvergence is retained and counted, not called infeasibility. Output still obeys the speed ball.

Pair requests are soft in this priority experiment: the wall repair can violate them. All controller calls count obstacle and pair residuals above 1e-7 m/s, including maxima. Wall residual includes the speed-ball excess. The exact last executed control-update command and constraints for contact-involved drones are saved; these come from before the contact, not recomputation at terminal state. Actual velocity at that control update is also saved. No causality or continuous-time invariance claim follows from these diagnostics.

Development cases reuse seed 1501 at 1,000 and 5,000, with existing baseline contact records kept. Evaluation seeds 1901/1902 are frozen before their runs. Large-flock windows are 20 seconds only. The separate 50-drone seed 1901 comparison runs the full 180-second mission. One candidate 1,000-drone refinement and a 50-drone full refinement use half the integration step. No candidate changes after protocol freeze, irrespective of results. A negative finding completes the experiment and must not be hidden.

Airflow is off, observations ideal, 100 Hz control, RK4 2.5 ms (refinement 1.25 ms), 27 g rigid bodies and 40 ms motor lag. Two numerical subprocesses may overlap; timings are not a performance benchmark. Three.js renders exact saved poses with visual interpolation. Physical validation, general safety, large-flock full passage and algorithmic novelty are not established here.

The first attempt passed a 17-component geometry normal into a 3-vector dot product, causing NaN. That implementation and attempted seeds 1801/1802 are retained in milestone-30-error-record. The corrected solver explicitly uses spatial components. Fresh evaluation seeds 1901/1902 replace the attempted seeds. This is a software correction before the new protocol freeze, not a controller gain change.

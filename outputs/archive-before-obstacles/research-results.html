What to look for

Start → 10 s: different directions become coordinated in the Boids view. 15–18 s: drones in the region x > 0 receive a sideways/upward steering cue. 25 s: compare how the group responds after the cue ends.

Direction agreement measures how similarly drones move (100% means parallel motion). Connected group counts the largest chain of neighbors within 1.8 m. Height spread shows that the group occupies a volume. These measurements describe behavior; they do not establish real-flight safety.

Across five fresh starting conditions, full Boids retains all 50 drones in one connected group and ends with 99.2–99.9% direction agreement. Separation-only ends at 13.8–28.4%, with its largest connected group containing 5–13 drones. Both complete 30 seconds without contact stops.

Research scope and evidence

Same initial states and six-degree-of-freedom drone physics in all views. Full Boids adds local separation, alignment and cohesion. Separation-only keeps repulsion but removes alignment and cohesion; all-off removes all three. Self-propulsion, soft environmental bounds and the local cue are identical. The replay shows seed 401. All Boids drones happen to occupy the cue region at 15 s in this replay; it does not establish propagation from a small informed subgroup. The initial cloud is already connected: this tests coordination and retention of connectivity, not merging disconnected flocks.

Soft bounds act beyond x/y ±8 m and altitude 2–7 m; they are steering responses, not solid walls. No positions are snapped or teleported. A separation below the assumed 15 cm contact threshold stops the whole run. Airflow and sensor errors are off. Steering is on the CPU. Three.js replays recorded physical states.

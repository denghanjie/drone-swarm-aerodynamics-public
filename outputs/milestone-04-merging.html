What the comparison does—and does not—establish

Both controllers use the same 50 initial states, vehicle mechanics, thrust limits, numerical steps and stopping criteria. The prior results are preserved. The new scheduler changes the navigation rule: it can use global vehicle states and discrete batch reservations, so this is not a comparison of two equally decentralized controllers.

Runs stop on first contact-envelope detection; impact dynamics are not simulated. Valid crossings must clear the opening by 7.5 cm. The study varies width (0.6, 1.2, 2.4 m), initial spacing (0.3, 0.6 m), speed (0.25, 0.5 m/s) and ten seeds. The displayed seed-1 examples use 0.6 m spacing and 0.5 m/s speed. Positions and attitudes are interpolated for smooth replay; calculations use finer time steps.

No airflow, wind, sensing error or communication delay is included. Numerical checks do not establish real-flight safety or a new research contribution. The initial-condition seeds are a repeatability study, not held-out physical validation.

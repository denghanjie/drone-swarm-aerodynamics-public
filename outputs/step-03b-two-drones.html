What this experiment means

Start aligned: the upper drone is held in hover. The lower drone starts at 1 m with its normal still-air motor setting. Under the constant ideal-power assumption, incoming air reduces lift. The lower drone sinks and its controller increases motor commands. The pale outline stays at the no-interaction reference.

Then change the offset: each of the four lower rotors samples a different part of the wake. Unequal lift creates roll or pitch. This is a one-way coupling: the lower drone does not change the upper drone or its wake. The controller regulates horizontal velocity, not horizontal position; the receiver may drift.

What is assumed: 27 g vehicles, 23 mm rotor radius and 56 mm adjacent rotor spacing. Wake coefficients come from a published hover model; transferring them to this size is unvalidated. Each motor state represents still-air-equivalent thrust, converted to ideal induced power and then to effective thrust in the incoming flow. This is not a measured motor law. Body drag, rotor disk averaging, vehicle-motion inflow, ground contact, turbulence and wake memory are omitted. Yaw torque retains the earlier proportional-thrust approximation.

Reading the scene: teal is effective lift; orange is weight. The upper blue drone is fixed. Rings show the source wake's half-speed radius, beginning at the model's far-field boundary. They are not a solid tube, a collision boundary or simulated smoke. Drones are drawn 2× larger; positions use meters.

Limits: the run stops if a rotor enters the excluded near field or the receiver tilts more than 20° (an exploratory cutoff, not a validated threshold). A negative altitude crosses only the reference grid. The existing 3D mechanics use 5 ms RK4 and 10 ms controller updates; CPU physics and Three.js WebGL playback.

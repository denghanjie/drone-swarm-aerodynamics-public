Understand the comparison

Three predictions, one scene: the solid receiver is the selected model. Purple wireframe shows the other model; pale wireframe is no interaction. The blue upper drone is fixed. Use the plot menu to compare altitude, pitch and sideways motion.

The controlled change: both models compute the same four wake-induced lift reductions at any identical state. “Uneven rotor loads” applies their total force and moments. “Total force only” applies the same total force along the receiver's lift axis but removes only the aerodynamic moment changes. Motor control torques remain active. This isolates the influence of uneven loading; it is not a faster implementation.

Why the forces later differ: once the models predict different positions and motor states, they sample different airflow. Equal force at identical states does not mean equal force along diverging trajectories. No interaction is computed separately with the same controller.

Scope: both wake models retain Step 3B's hypothetical constant ideal induced-power response, 27 g vehicles, assumed geometry and one-way stationary wake. Neither is experimentally validated. The controller regulates horizontal velocity, not position. Drones are drawn 2× larger; the grid has no collisions. Rotor lift readouts are diagnostic in total-force mode, where their individual moments are deliberately discarded.

Fair comparison window: playback and plots end when either model reaches its limit, or at 16 s. No stopped trajectory is extended or treated as a successful full run. The 20° tilt limit is an exploratory cutoff, not a physical safety boundary. Read the comparison results.

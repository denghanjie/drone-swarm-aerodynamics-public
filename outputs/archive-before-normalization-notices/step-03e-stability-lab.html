Step 3E · What did we learn?

1. Find a balance point. We solved for a hover where forces and moments balance and the controller agrees with the motor commands. This point is not the requested location: steady errors remain because this controller has no integral correction.

2. Give it a tiny nudge. These stability numbers refer to the default 0.45 m gap, 0.06 m offset and 2.25× thrust setting; they do not recompute with the sliders. We calculate how a small disturbance changes over each 0.01-second controller cycle. The largest mode multiplier is 1.01984 with the original gains and 1.01704 with the selected gains. Above 1 means some small disturbances grow. Both tested wake-hover equilibria are locally unstable in this model. Without the wake, the corresponding hover is locally stable.

3. Try a fixed gain study. We compared 27 gain sets at one geometry, using a declared score that includes position, altitude, speed and tilt over all 16 seconds. The winner halves horizontal position feedback (1 → 0.5 s⁻¹) and doubles angular-rate damping (20 → 40 s⁻¹). Other gains stay fixed.

4. Read the tradeoff. At the default geometry, peak pitch falls from 16.85° to 9.16°, but horizontal RMS error grows from 11.57 cm to 14.51 cm. Six configurations excluded from gain selection test transfer; the low-thrust case still exceeds the exploratory tilt limit. “Selected” means best in this finite grid under this score, not a stable or optimal controller.

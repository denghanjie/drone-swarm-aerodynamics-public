Understand this experiment

3D flight sequence: hover to 2 s; move along X to 5 s; move along Y to 8 s; brake and climb to 1.8 m; turn 90° at 11 s. The orange nose points along the drone's forward axis. Watch pitch when moving along X, roll when moving along Y, and yaw when turning. The light grid is a reference plane, not a collision surface.

Real dynamics, separate playback: four motor thrusts produce total lift plus roll, pitch and yaw torques. The rigid body responds through Newton–Euler equations and a quaternion orientation. A deterministic 16-second run is computed on the CPU when settings change, then played back. Three.js renders that state in 3D. This is not yet a WebGPU compute benchmark.

Scope: CF2X reference mass, inertia and motor geometry; assumed 40 ms motor delay, controller gains and 15° requested-tilt limit. No drag, wind, downwash, sensing error, battery model or collision response. A yaw torque is modeled from rotor reaction torque. Rotor animation is schematic, not a rotor-speed measurement. Zero horizontal speed is requested after a maneuver; horizontal position is not locked.

Verification: 30 automated checks cover free fall, roll/pitch acceleration, motor mixing, yaw, angular momentum conservation, thrust bounds and smaller timesteps. These are numerical checks, not validation against measured flight. Read the experiment record.

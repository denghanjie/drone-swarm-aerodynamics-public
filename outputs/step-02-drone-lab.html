What this experiment models

One 27 g CF2X-inspired drone moves in a vertical plane. Two motor groups create total thrust and a turning moment. Gravity, pitch inertia, per-group thrust limits and a first-order motor response determine the motion. Commands update at 100 Hz; physics uses a 5 ms fixed step with fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration. Playback shows a precomputed deterministic run, independent of browser frame rate.

Mass, pitch inertia and rotor positions come from the documented CF2X reference. The 40 ms motor response, controller gains and 15° requested-tilt limit are explicit experimental assumptions. No drag, wakes, contact, sensing noise, roll or yaw are included. The tilt request is limited; actual tilt follows the dynamics. Hover starts already airborne with motors at hover thrust.

Move & brake: hover for 2 s; request 0.5 m/s until 6 s; then request zero speed. Zero-speed control stops the drone where it ends up; it does not return it to the starting position. Climb requests a change from 1 m to 3 m at 2 s. A motor command limit means a requested thrust was clipped to the available range.

Seventeen numerical checks passed, including analytical free fall, tilted acceleration, motor response and timestep refinement. They verify the equations and implementation, not agreement with real flight. Source: CF2X reference parameters.

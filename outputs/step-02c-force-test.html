Understand this experiment

1. Before 4 s: both runs hover at 1 m. 2. From 4 to 8 s: an extra downward force acts on one drone. It sinks, and the controller raises motor thrust. 3. After 8 s: the push ends. With the default settings, the drone returns to 1 m. The pale wireframe marks the unloaded reference, which stays at 1 m. The magenta arrow is the extra force; orange is weight and teal is thrust.

Why does it settle below the target? This controller adds thrust in proportion to altitude error and opposes vertical velocity. It has no integral correction. For a load fraction L, the predicted steady altitude loss is L × 9.81 / 6 meters, provided thrust is sufficient. At 20%, this is 0.327 m. This is a prediction for a sustained load; our pulse lasts four seconds.

What we are testing: the same full 3D rigid-body mechanics and 40 ms motor response as Step 2B, with a known world-vertical force at the center of mass. The test is symmetric, so motion is vertical even though the underlying model is 3D. Drone drawings are enlarged 6×. The grid has no physical contact. CPU integration uses 5 ms steps and 10 ms controller updates; Three.js renders playback with WebGL.

What this establishes: correct response to prescribed force and control limits, not experimentally validated downwash. There is no wake, drag, airflow torque, ground effect or collision response. Read the experiment record.
